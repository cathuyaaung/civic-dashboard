3. Item 6.1 - Staff Presentation BL.09-NBR All Wards.pdf
Neighbourhood Commercial: Solutions at Home
File: BL.09-NBR (All Wards)
Roberto Vertolli, Planner, presented an overview of the report, including its intent, potential neighbourhood benefits, implementation considerations, Official Plan policies, zoning by-law regulations, and avenues of investigation related to home occupations and neighbourhood-scale uses.
Councillor Tedjo spoke to home occupations and private home-based businesses, suggesting that consideration be given to a more flexible and progressive regulatory framework. Councillor Tedjo noted that the scope could extend beyond single detached dwellings and include broader categories, such as food-based services, retail and restaurants, where appropriate. Councillor Tedjo emphasized the importance of defining suitable locations, including mapping areas across the City, and inquired about timelines for bringing the report back to the Committee. Andrew Whittemore, Commissioner, Planning and Building, advised that timelines would depend on the scope of work and the direction provided by the Committee. R. Vertolli added that street-specific regulations and filters were outlined in the report.
Councillor Butt inquired about the potential role of the Committee of Adjustment and whether the proposed framework could increase minor variance applications. Mr. Vertolli responded that any permitted uses and standards would be established through the zoning framework approved by Council, noting that the extent of impacts would depend on the scope approved. Councillor Butt suggested a 2027 timeline with full public consultation.
Mayor Parrish and several Councillors raised questions regarding benchmarking with other municipalities, potential area exclusions, impacts on neighbourhoods versus arterial roads, and concerns related to traffic, parking, odours, licensing, and compatibility, particularly for restaurants and medical offices. Planning staff advised that limited data had been reviewed, filtering would be applied, and public engagement would be a key component of the process once direction was confirmed.
Committee Members emphasized the need for robust public consultation, clear definitions, and careful consideration of location-based approaches. Suggestions included separating neighbourhood and arterial road permissions, exploring a pilot approach, and deferring the work until after the municipal election.
Following discussion, Councillor Dasko suggested deferral of the report, with planning staff to return at that time for direction on which categories Council wishes to explore.
RECOMMENDATION PDC-0023-2026
Moved By Councillor S. Dasko
That the report dated April 22, 2026, entitled “Neighbourhood Commercial: Solutions at Home”, from the Commissioner of Planning and Building, be deferred to next term of Council in 2027.
YES (10)
Councillor S. Dasko, Councillor C. Fonseca, Councillor J. Kovac, Councillor D. Damerla, Councillor M. Mahoney, Councillor S. McFadden, Councillor J. Horneck, Councillor M. Reid, Councillor B. Butt, and Councillor N. Hart
NO (1)
Councillor A. Tedjo
Carried (10 to 1)

6. Item 6.2 Staff Presentation - MOP2051 Housekeeping Amendments.pdf
Mississauga Official Plan 2051 (MOP 2051) – Provincial Modifications and “Housekeeping” Amendments
Amina Menkad, Planner, presented an overview of the report, including the ministerial modifications, alignment with provincial priorities, the requested deferral for the Dixie‑Dundas Specific Policy Area, and MOP 2051 housekeeping amendments.
Councillor Damerla inquired about the rationale for the modifications and whether any developer inquiries had been made at the ministry level. Ben Phillips, Executive Manager, Official Plan and City‑Wide Initiatives, advised that no rationale was provided in the written decision and noted that landowners had contacted the Province of Ontario after the Official Plan was posted on the City’s website regarding matters related to building height increases and the Specific Policy Area.
Councillor Tedjo inquired how the modifications interact with recent provincial legislation. In response, A. Menkad explained that prior regulatory changes affected Inclusionary Zoning and resulted in a freeze on Overlay Zones, and that subsequent ministerial changes related to rates and the freeze have impacted the City of Mississauga.
RECOMMENDATION PDC-0024-2026
Moved By Councillor B. Butt
That the proposed amendments to Mississauga Official Plan 2051 (MOP 2051), as detailed in the report dated April 22, 2026 from the Commissioner of Planning and Building be approved in accordance with the following:
That the necessary official plan amendment by-laws in substantial conformance with Appendix 3 and Appendix 4 be enacted at a future City Council Meeting.
That notwithstanding planning protocol, the report regarding proposed amendments to MOP 2051 be considered both the public meeting and combined information and recommendation report.

2025 Growth Monitoring Report
Nadia Dowhaniuk, Planner, spoke to the purpose of the report, including matters related to new residential development, building permit activity, and the proposed framework for housing growth.
Councillor Butt inquired whether the Region of Peel was involved in the growth management process. J. Bevan responded that local municipalities have the authority, noting that the Region of Peel’s involvement was limited.
Mayor Parrish raised concerns related to Additional Residential Unit (ARU) permits and advised that complaints have been received regarding the processing of backyard units. In response, Andrew Whittemore, Commissioner of Planning and Building, noted that the process can be challenging for planning staff, particularly when members of the public do not use the pre-approved program. A. Whittemore further advised that planning staff are currently working to expedite the process.
