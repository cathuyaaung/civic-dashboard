7.5
Layan Elsaqqa, and Asma Alsebai, Community Members, Seeds of Leadership, with respect to Animal Shelter Overcrowding in Mississauga Summary
L. Elsaqqa and A. Alsebai spoke to increased animal shelter overcrowding in the City, noting challenges affecting pet ownership, systemic pressures, and operational challenges, and spoke to the impacts of overcrowding on animals and staff.
L. Elsaqqa and A. Alsebai proposed considerations for expanding preventative supports to reduce shelter intake, highlighting pet retention programs, pet food bank programs, annual surveys to assess community needs, and continued advocacy for pet-inclusive housing policies.
Mayor Parrish commented on pet retention strategies and preventative approaches and requested that staff benchmark and explore best practices in other jurisdictions.
Committee Members commented on the following:
Renovated shelter facilities
Role of external organizations
Need to support service capacity
Enabling residents to retain pets where possible
Existing supports through Food Banks Mississauga
Waiving surrender fees
Raj Sheth, Commissioner, Community Services spoke to Animal Services' building updates and Georgios Fthenos, Director, Enforcement spoke to shelter capacity, success rates of adoption and reunification, expanding community engagement and outreach program and upcoming events and programming.
Direction was provided to Enforcement staff to conduct benchmarking on other municipalities regarding pet retention and support programs.

10.4
Noise Control By-law – Proposed Amendments to Support Live Music and Transition from the Live Music Pilot
1. Noise Control By-law - Proposed Amendments to Support Live Music and Transition from the Live Music Pilot.pdf
2. Appendix 1 - Cultural Districts Maps1.pdf
3. Appendix 2 - Survey Results_Noise Control By-law Live Music Pilot.pdf
This item was considered under item 7.8
RECOMMENDATION GC-0280-2026
Moved By Councillor S. Dasko
That the report dated May 4, 2026, entitled “Noise Control By-law – Proposed Amendments to Support Live Music and Transition from the Live Music Pilot” from the Commissioner of Planning and Building be approved.
That the Noise Control By-law 0369-1979, as amended, be further amended, effective June 29, 2026, to:
Update the definition of “live music” to clarify it includes acoustic and amplified performances and excludes the playback of recorded music without active singing or use of an instrument;
Expand the permitted areas for live music to all City Cultural Districts, subject to specified permitted times, sound limits, business licence types, and event permits outlined in Table A of this report; and
Update provisions related to sound measurement (point of reception) and clarify prohibited time periods for noise arising from musical instruments in residential areas and Quiet Zones.
That Legal Services be authorized to make an application to the Ministry of the Attorney General for set fines under Part I of the Provincial Offences Act in the amount of $305 for contraventions of the new requirements being added to the Noise Control By-law 0369-1979, as amended.
That all necessary by-laws be enacted.
That Staff be directed to implement the communications plan outlined in this report following amendment of the Noise Control By-law 360-1979, as amended.
YES (11)
Councillor S. Dasko, Councillor C. Fonseca, Councillor J. Kovac, Mayor Parrish, Councillor D. Damerla, Councillor S. McFadden, Councillor A.Tedjo, Councillor J. Horneck, Councillor M. Reid, Councillor B. Butt, and Councillor N. Hart
