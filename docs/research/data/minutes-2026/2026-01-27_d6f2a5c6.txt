6.1
Michael Jackman, Resident, regarding Signal Priority on Hazel McCallion Light Rail Transit
Michael Jackman, resident, spoke to the importance and benefits of rapid transit, including the Hazel McCallion Line and planned BRT projects. M. Jackman highlighted ongoing challenges related to transit signal priority, delays, and impacts on ridership. Carolyn Ryall, Director, Rapid Transit Program Office, thanked the resident for the deputation and noted that the City is working closely with Metrolinx and Mobilinx on transit signal priority. C. Ryall advised that further updates will be provided to the Committee and Council.
Committee Members engaged in the discussion of the following:
Final decision-making authority regarding signal operations
Signal performance and planned future stops
Final signal configuration
Signal governance model
Sensitivity around signal priority
C. Ryall advised that the project agreement was between Metrolinx, the Province, and the City of Mississauga and committed to reporting back with further information. C. Ryall stated that significant work is ongoing throughout the corridor and that no information has yet been shared regarding the final operation of the system. More details will be provided as they become available.

6.2
Suzanne Holder, Manager, MiWay Customer Experience and Innovation and Jeannette Ladd, Anishinaabe-kwe Artist, regarding City Centre Transit Terminal Public Art Installation
1. Item 6.2 Staff Presentation - City Centre Transit Terminal Public Art Installation.pdf
Maureen Cosyn Heath, Director, Transit, presented an update on the two City‑commissioned murals for the City Centre Transit terminal, outlining the project objectives, artist selection process, and community engagement. M. Cosyn Heath introduced artist Jeannette Ladd as the selected muralist. Jeannette Ladd spoke about the mural’s design, meaning, and its importance within the transit system. Maureen thanked the artist for her participation and contributions.
Committee Members expressed appreciation for the artwork and thanked the artist for helping to make the terminal a more welcoming space. Committee Members noted that the artwork will enhance quality of life for riders, suggesting that additional art installations in other terminals may also be beneficial. Questions were raised regarding the selection process, the mural protection against vandalism, and the format of the art display. M. Cosyn Heath and C. Ryall responded to the questions.

6.3
Suzanne Holder, Manager, MiWay Customer Experience and Innovation, regarding Customer Communications Tool
1. Item 6.3 Staff Presentation - Customer Communcations Tool.pdf
Maureen Cosyn Heath, Director, Transit, spoke to customer satisfaction as the key driver analysis, communications reliability, customer feedback, future focused communications and the communications platform.
Councillor Damerla joined the meeting at 6:47pm. Councillor Damerla inquired about the cost of the two‑year pilot, the approval process, and whether the report would be brought back to Council. Maureen Cosyn Heath, Director, Transit, responded to the questions. Councillor Damerla left the meeting at 7:30pm.
Committee Members engaged in the discussion of the following:
Pilot Launch timeline
Temporary service application for riders
Features of the new applications (real-time reporting and data, accessibility specific alerts, etc.)
If MiWay will still be accessible though City's website
Real-time screen installation at UTM and students' feedback of the platform
Impact on the riders with accessibility needs given that the app will be discontinued.
MiWay staff confirmed that the pilot will launch in the first half of 2026 and that they are working with Metrolinx to extend Triplinx while exploring interim solutions to prevent service gaps. The new platform will include real‑time data and allow riders to receive personalized information, with accessibility features continuing to be developed. MiWay staff clarified that the app will not replace Google Maps, which would still offer limited trip‑planning, and noted that Transit 55 was not affiliated with MiWay. A digital signage contract has been signed, with the option to add screens at UTM. MiWay staff also acknowledged concerns about continuity and reiterated their efforts with Metrolinx to maintain consistent service.
RECOMMENDATION TAC-0003-2026
Moved By D. Fisher
That the deputation from Maureen Cosyn Heath, Director of Transit, regarding Customer Communications Tool, be received for information.
(TAC-0003-2026)
Received
6.4
Maureen Cosyn Heath, Director of Transit, regarding MiWay Multiyear Business Plan Highlights
1. Item 6.4 Staff Presentation - MiWay Multiyear Business Plan Highlights.pdf
Maureen Cosyn Heath, Director, Transit spoke to highlights of the MiWay Business Plan of 2026-2029, key objectives and services, 2026 operating budget and 10-year capital budget summary.
J. Giggs, Citizen Member, inquired about projected 2025 boardings and the potential impact on service levels. M. Cosyn Heath reported that ridership was down 10% compared to 2024 and noted that, through regular service planning, MiWay ensured adequate service levels for 2025, maintaining sufficient capacity and avoiding overcrowding.
D. Fisher, Citizen Member, requested an update on the bus replacement program. M. Cosyn Heath advised that staff were currently in discussions with vendors to determine next steps for upcoming bus replacements.
M. Song, Citizen Member, inquired which projects were included under the higher‑order transit budget line. M. Cosyn Heath clarified that this category included initiatives such as the Dundas Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) project.
M. Ahmad, Citizen Member, requested clarification on the Item “Buildings” budget line. M. Cosyn Heath explained that the “Buildings” category comprised various infrastructure components, including bus terminals and related facilities.

11.
OTHER BUSINESS
D. Fisher, Citizen Member, raised concerns regarding snow clearing at bus stops, asking how accessibility was being maintained during winter conditions and whether there will be bus stop replacements along Hurontario. M. Cosyn Heath advised that bus stops are generally plowed within 24 hours; if a stop is not cleared, riders are encouraged to report it. C. Ryall explained that Mobilinx has begun the first phase of shelter implementation.
M. Ahmad, Citizen Member, inquired about the status of enhanced transit shelters, including heating, accessibility improvements, and the Central Parkway terminal project. M. Cosyn Heath advised that MiWay staff are working with Alectra on shelter upgrades and that an update on the terminal project will be provided at a future Transit Advisory Committee meeting. Discussion also addressed the former Region of Peel affordable transit pass, with M. Cosyn Heath noting there are no current plans to change the program and that the question of affordable mobile data will be taken back. J. Horneck confirmed that social services will remain with the Region of Peel.
M. Song, Citizen Member, requested clarification on which projects the Rapid Transit Office will lead and raised concerns about the cost and delivery timeline of the Dundas BRT, referencing comparisons to Waterloo. C. Ryall responded that multiple projects are underway, including Dundas BRT and Lakeshore BRT, with Lakeshore currently in the design phase and procurement to follow. C. Ryall noted ongoing coordination with Metrolinx and MTO and advised that while definitive timelines are not available due to project complexity, efforts are being made to improve delivery where possible.
