DEPUTATIONS
7.1
Monica Vaswani, Warning Preparedness Meteorologist, Meteorological Service of Canada, Environment and Climate Change Canada regarding weather forecasting and alert processes
M. Vaswani spoke to severe thunderstorm forecasting and challenges associated with forecasting heavy rainfall, highlighting:
Meteorological ingredients required for thunderstorm development
Limitations of weather models at localized scales
Role of radar, including sources of rainfall underestimation
Use of colour‑coded alert systems based on forecast confidence and anticipated impacts
Challenges from lake breeze boundaries and training thunderstorms
Committee Members commented and inquired about the following:
The geographic scale at which coloured weather alerts are issued
Planned implementation of polygon‑based thunderstorm warnings
Radar limitations related to distance from radar stations
Ability to assess year‑over‑year storm frequency and intensity
How climate data and “100‑year storm” terminology is defined and updated
Methods for residents or partners to submit storm observations to Environment and Climate Change Canada
M. Vaswani responded to questions, and Scott Perry, Manager, Stormwater Assets & Programming spoke to design standards, IDF curves, and infrastructure design assumptions.
Councillor Dasko requested that information be shared regarding how residents can submit storm observations to support forecasting and data collection; in response, M. Vaswani noted they would follow up with the requested information.
RECOMMENDATION SWAC-0007-2026
Moved By Mritunjay Mallik
That the deputation and associated presentation from Monica Vaswani, Warning Preparedness Meteorologist, Meteorological Service of Canada, Environment and Climate Change Canada regarding weather forecasting and alert processes, be received.
Received
7.2
Nick Lorrain, Senior Manager, Flood Risk Management and Rita Lucero, Manager, Flood Emergency Management, Toronto and Region Conservation Authority regarding flood plain mapping, forecasting, mitigation, and messaging systems
N. Lorrain spoke to the Toronto and Region Conservation Authority’s (TRCA) flood risk management program, flood plain mapping methodologies and flood definitions, flood forecasting and warning systems, and flood mitigation infrastructure planning and environmental assessment processes.
R. Lucero spoke to TRCA’s flood risk outreach initiatives for the public and highlighted the public flood message subscription service.
Committee Members commented and inquired about the following:
Responsibility for mapping urban overland flooding within municipalities
Whether the City intends to undertake comprehensive overland flood mapping
How new development outfalls are assessed and approved
Capacity limitations of existing stormwater infrastructure
Construction-phase flood risk and mitigation measures
Whether flood alerts can be customized to specific stream gauges
Whether flood alerts can escalate between severity categories
Opportunities to expand public awareness and subscription to flood alerts
In response, N. Lorrain and R. Lucero addressed questions regarding floodplain mapping roles, flood alert practices, and current limitations related to public flood messaging customization. Scott Perry, Manager, Stormwater Assets & Programming spoke to impermeable surfaces, overland flooding, and existing online stormwater resources for the public.
Councillor Fonseca, Chair requested that TRCA share information related to outreach, resources, or tools available for residents living adjacent to ravines to support awareness, preparedness, and engagement. In response, N. Lorrain, noted they would followup with available information.
RECOMMENDATION SWAC-0008-2026
Moved By Mark Anthony Del Gobbo
That the deputation and associated presentation from Nick Lorrain, Senior Manager, Flood Risk Management and Rita Lucero, Manager, Flood Emergency Management, Toronto and Region Conservation Authority regarding flood plain mapping, forecasting, mitigation, and messaging systems, be received.
Received

10.4
Transit Programs Partnership in the Dixie-Dundas Flood Mitigation Project
Paul Bunyard, Citizen Member asked questions related to the project design and construction timelines, integration between the Dundas BRT and flood mitigation works, and public access to project schedule and updates.
In response, Scott Perry, Manager, Stormwater Assets & Programming and Lincoln Kan, Manager, Environmental Services spoke to the project webpage reflecting updated design progress and schedules.
Mike Gaudet, Citizen Member spoke to how residents south of Little Etobicoke Creek could face increased flood risk during the construction period and inquired about interim flood protection measures. In response, S. Perry noted that appropriate construction phasing and safeguards would be considered.
Councillor Fonseca, Chair spoke to the importance of the project webpage as a public communication tool and suggested adding a column to the project schedule to clearly indicate project phases. In response, L. Kan noted that the webpage has been updated to include clearer scheduling and that it will continue to be updated as the project progresses.
Councillor Fonseca, Chair asked whether, if the Committee or Councillors receive inquiries from a specific neighbourhood, school, or residents’ association, the City could offer a community public house. In response, L. Kan noted that the project manager’s contact information is available on the webpage and that where requested, staff could accommodate community‑specific engagement opportunities.
RECOMMENDATION SWAC-0013-2026
Moved By Paul Bunyard
That the memorandum from Anthony Di Giandomenico, Storm Drainage Engineer, Infrastructure Planning & Engineering Services, dated April 15, 2026, entitled “Transit Programs Partnership in the Dixie-Dundas Flood Mitigation Project”, be received.
Received

OTHER BUSINESS
Mark Anthony Del Gobbo, Citizen Member spoke to and inquired about rainfall intensity duration frequency (IDF) curves, how often they are reviewed, whether current standards are being benchmarked against other Ontario municipalities, whether recent climate trends are being incorporated into design storm updates, and how legacy infrastructure is being addressed.
In response, Scott Perry, Manager, Stormwater Assets & Programming and Lincoln Kan, Manager, Environmental Services provided information on IDF curves, minor storm system, infrastructure capacity regarding extreme storm events, and upgrades to infrastructure.
Paul Bunyard, Citizen Member spoke to minor and major systems and whether changing conditions have been assessed, and current studies related to the 16 Mile Creek area; in response, S. Perry provided information.
Councillor Fonseca, Chair spoke to concerns about private companies soliciting residents regarding flood mitigation rebates, what actions residents can take to respond, and City‑issued information that could be shared with the public and Councillors.
L. Kan noted the City is aware and advised the City does not conduct door-to-door solicitation and that efforts to communicate are underway. S. Perry advised that residents contact City staff directly for accurate information on rebate programs.
