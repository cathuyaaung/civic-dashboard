7.1
Martin Tsvetanov, Resident, with respect to Climate Change Action Plan and Sign By-law 0054-2002
1. City Presentation Martin Tsvetanov.pdf
Martin Tsvetanov, Resident identified an advertisement on a billboard located in the city that was placed by an organization and lobby group for several large oil companies and expressed concerns with the messaging regarding the economic benefits of a pipeline infrastructure but fails to mention the environmental damages associated with pipelines. Mr. Tsvetanov proposed that this type of advertising ought not be permitted in accordance with the City's Advertising and Sponsorship Policy. Mr. Tsvetanov outlined applicable sections of the Sign By-law-0054-2002 and criteria and guidelines contained in the Advertising and Sponsorship Policy and provided examples of the city’s strategic goals that conflict with fossil fuel advertising and that warrant prohibiting such type of advertising.
Graham Walsh, City Solicitor and Commissioner, Legislative Services, noted that there are no guidelines in the Sign By-law to review content during the review of an application; however, should Council wish to consider its role in reviewing content Mr. Walsh suggests staff report back on a more detailed legal analysis on the role municipal governments have when reviewing content for private signs on private lands.
Councillor Horneck, spoke to the complaints he has received from residents on certain advertisements and agrees that a full analysis, that includes the rules in other jurisdictions, be conducted.
In response to Mayor Parrish's suggestion that there be a quick study of the matter, Graham Walsh, City Solicitor and Commissioner, Legislative Services, advised that staff will report back in at a closed session.
RECOMMENDATION GC-0001-2026
Moved By Councillor J. Horneck
That the deputation and associated presentation by Martin Tsvetanov with respect to Climate Change and Sign By-law 0054-2002, be received.
That Legal staff report back to Committee with an analysis of municipal government's role and legal issues regarding private signs on private lands and benchmarking of what criteria/rules exist.
Received

12.4
Letter dated January 5, 2026 from Sharon Crowe, Director of Legal Services, Mississauga Community Legal Services, with respect to Bill 60 "Fighting Delays, Building Faster Act, 2025"
1. Bill 60 Advocacy Letter to General Council.pdf
Councillor Tedjo shared the concerns expressed by staff at the Mississauga Community Legal Services with respect to the amendments to the Landlord and Tenant Board under Bill 60, and noted that although Bill 60 has been adopted, the Ministry is still working on Regulation amendments. Councillor Tedjo sought direction in terms of advocacy on behalf of residents.
In response to Councillor Tedjo's enquiry regarding whether staff have prepared an analysis of the Regulation amendments and how it would affect tenants, and whether there is a communication plan to advise residents of changes and challenges, Jason Bevan, Director, City Planning Strategies advised that during the consultation period staff presented its report to Council that identified the changes and the areas of concern, primarily with the seven day eviction notice period and the lack of means to address the backlog. Mr. Bevan noted that staff would prepare a report back to Committee on staff and Committee's comments on the Regulations and further noted that he is not aware of the timelines for the Regulations to be enacted.
Councillor Tedjo suggested that the Mayor send a letter to the Ministry to outline the challenges for the municipality and its residents.
RECOMMENDATION GC-0010-2026
Moved By Councillor A.Tedjo
That the letter dated January 5, 2026 from Sharon Crowe, Director of Legal Services, Mississauga Community Legal Services, with respect to Bill 60 "Fighting Delays, Building Faster Act, 2025" be received.
That planning staff report back to Committee on staff and Committee's comments on the Regulations.
That the Mayor send a letter to the Ministry to outline the challenges for the municipality and its residents.
Received
13.
MATTERS PERTAINING TO REGION OF PEEL COUNCIL
Councillor Kovac spoke to the issues residents have experienced with the startup of the new service provider for waste collection.
Mayor Parrish spoke to the need to reach out the Region regarding their lack of response and communication to residents regarding the issues they were experiencing with the new waste collection.
In response to Mayor Parrish's enquiry regarding the cost savings for the transfer from the Region to the new waste collection company, Councillor Damerla explained that the cost savings was reflected as a onetime savings in the 2024 budget.
Councillor Horneck spoke to the issues residents experienced and the lack of communication from the service provider on the changes to the content of materials eligible for recycling.
Councillor Fonseca spoke to concerns residents have expressed regarding the lack of communication from the service provider regarding whether townhouse complex residents will remain on recycling bag collection as an alternative to recycling bins due to space restrictions; lack of service; changes to content of materials eligible for recycling and what residents do when a recycling bin is broken and whether there is a cost to replace a broken recycling bin. Councillor Fonseca noted an opportunity to speak at an upcoming session with Members of Provincial Parliament (MPP) regarding these concerns as well as concerns relating to Bill 60.
Deputy Mayor and Councillor Mahoney, expressed concerns regarding items that may not be eligible for recycling which will increase the amount thrown in the garbage.
Mayor Parrish noted that with the closing of the beer stores, there has been no communication of what will be done with all the bottles.
Mayor Parrish requested that Rob Trewartha, Director, Strategic Communications & Initiatives provide Members of Committee with a summary of what we are submitting as our request to the province in advance of our meeting with the MPP's.
