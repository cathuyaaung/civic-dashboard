Ridgeway Plaza Interim Control By-law Study and Zoning By-law Amendment
File: CD.03-RID W8
Trista James, Planner provided an overview of the subject lands, recommendation report, recommended zoning approach, key proposed zoning regulations and legally existing uses.
Councillor Tedjo expressed appreciation for the efforts of Planning staff, Councillor and Deputy Mayor Matt Mahoney, and Councillor Sue McFadden. Councillor Tedjo noted that, based on the presentation, there appeared to be some misunderstanding regarding the proposed 50% reduction in gross floor area (GFA). Councillor Tedjo inquired if this reduction would apply to existing businesses, whether future businesses would be subject to the cap, requesting additional information regarding the 50% limitation. It was further noted that owners could apply to the Committee of Adjustment for an exemption if necessary.
Jordan Lee, Manager, Zoning Initiatives, clarified that the maximum GFA does not apply to individual units but rather to the entire site.
The limitation would come into effect depending on changes in land use, such as restaurants going out of business. If a change involves the same facility, such as a restaurant purchase and change in ownership, the limitation would not apply. Similarly, if an adjacent unit was combined as a restaurant, this would not be impacted until the overall site falls below the cap. J. Lee emphasized that this was not a permanent freeze on expansions. As conditions on the site change, City staff will monitor and review the impact and remain available to reassess as needed.
B. Butt inquired about enforcement, questioning whether compliance would rely on businesses applying for zoning permits and what the compliance rate might be. Councillor Butt mentioned the previous 60-metre setback requirement for restaurant zones and inquired why this provision was no longer in effect, noting prior experience as a member of the Committee of Adjustment.
Jordan Lee, Manager, Zoning Initiatives, responded that obtaining a business license requires a certificate of occupancy, and any illegally established businesses would constitute an enforcement issue. While all units currently have valid certificates of occupancy, some may not be in compliance. Should Council approve the by-law, staff will create an inventory of legally established sites for enforcement purposes. In the case of this particular site, the 60-metre setback would not apply due to the right-of-way and would not resolve issues or trigger variances.
Councillor Parrish expressed disagreement with the removal of the 60-metre setback and suggested that it may be beneficial to review this provision, noting that the Committee of Adjustment would evaluate the sites when reviewing applications.
Councillor and Deputy Mayor Mahoney requested that staff bring forward a review of the 60-metre setback and confirmed that staff are working on this matter. Councillor Mahoney emphasized the goal of achieving a proper balance, ensuring sufficient parking and safety within the plaza over the long term. Councillor and Deputy Mayor Mahoney expressed his appreciation to staff and Councillor McFadden for their significant efforts, noting that staff and Councillors are striving to balance the needs of the community and the plaza.
Harneha Gulati, a business owner, expressed concerns with respect to imposing limitations on existing businesses. H. Gulati requested to reconsider and revise the proposal through collaboration with business owners.
Councillor Mahoney requested clarification from staff regarding whether the proposed GFA reduction would retroactively apply to existing businesses. It was confirmed that existing restaurants would not be affected by the cap. Councillor Mahoney noted that he remains available to discuss the matter offline with any interested parties.
Trista James, Planner, advised that existing businesses will continue operating as they are, with no retroactive repercussions.
Mayor Parrish reiterated that current businesses will not experience any reduction in GFA.
Gursharn Singh, a business owner, requested additional time to consider the recommendation and sought clarification on the status of legally existing businesses.
Councillor and Deputy Mayor Mahoney responded that the Interim Control By-law (ICBL) process began approximately a year and a half ago and included public meetings. Councillo Mahoney emphasized that the intent was to be thoughtful and achieve balance, encouraging anyone with questions to contact either Councillors or City staff.
Nicholas Dell, representing Harper and Dell Planning Consultants and acting on behalf of PSCC 1140 and PSCC 1136 condominium corporations, thanked Councillor Mahoney and City staff for their efforts. Mr. Dell noted that minor variances would conflict with the proposed cap.
Councillor Mahoney explained that minor variances were currently before the Committee of Adjustment, citing challenges such as insufficient garbage receptacles and parking concerns, with approximately 300 parking spaces involved.Councillor and Deputy Mayor Mahoney acknowledged that navigating the process was complex.
Iram Kashif, speaking on behalf of the Ridgeway Business Association, thanked planning staff for clarifying the GFA provisions. I. Kashif expressed willingness on behalf of the association to assist with communication efforts. Mr. Kashif noted that most restaurants were family-owned and served local families, emphasizing that nuisances did not originate from customers but rather from traffic and enforcement issues. Mr. Kashif stated that business owners do not support the proposed cap, as it would negatively impact businesses, and suggested forming a Business Improvement Area (BIA) in order to collaborate with the City.
Aysha Mitha, speaking on behalf of the Ridgeway Business Association, highlighted the importance of protecting the community and supporting local employment, calling for increased communication and engagement.
Eram Ansari, a business owner, reiterated the association’s commitment to working with City staff and Councillors to develop a mutual solution, noting the diversity of customers who frequent the plaza.
Councillor and Deputy Mayor Mahoney thanked all the speakers, noting he is committed to working proactively with condominium corporations and business owners. Councillor McFadden also expressed appreciation for the speakers and written submissions, stating a willingness to visit Ridgeway Plaza to meet with business owners directly.

PUBLIC MEETING RECOMMENDATION REPORT (WARD 1)
1. Information and Recommendation Report - OZ 25-19 W1 - 50 High Street East - 0024-2026.pdf

3. Item 6.2 Staff Presentation - OZ 25-19 W1.pdf
Rezoning application to permit an 11 storey apartment building containing 96 dwelling units
50 High Street East, north of High Street East, west of Hurontario Street
Owner: 1001107627 Ontario Inc.
File: OZ 25-19 W1
Lucas Petricca, Planner, provided an overview of the subject property, including the site plan, renderings, official plan policies, Zoning By-law provisions, evaluation, and staff recommendation.
Tony De Franco, Lead Planner from Sajecki Planning Inc., noted that the project has been developed through a collaborative process with the City since last spring, including a community meeting held in June. T. De Franco advised that the site plan approval application and demolition process were currently under review by City staff. Tony De Franco emphasized the importance of making a decision, as having zoning in place would facilitate the project. Mr. De Franco acknowledged the hard work of both City and Regional staff.
Tim Neeb, landowner stated that the development would provide housing for medium-income residents, with 10 units designated for homecoming groups. Mr. Neeb invited members to visit the site, noting that the units resemble high-quality condominiums.
Mayor Parrish inquired whether the affordability of the project is due to City incentives. Mr. Neeb confirmed that this was the case.
Nicholas Dell, representing Harper Dell and serving as Board President of PCC 37, expressed support for the development. Mr. Dell raised concerns regarding construction practices, particularly related to elevation and underground levels, and requested an assessment to ensure safe construction given the proximity of an adjacent building that is approximately fifty years old.
Councillor Dasko thanked the community for its consultation and feedback, as well as the Town of Port Credit Association. Councillor Dasko noted that development projects often take different forms and acknowledged that initial plans lacked visitor parking, which was subsequently addressed through collaboration with the owners. Councillor Dasko highlighted efforts to ensure safety in the surrounding area, including resolving issues with garbage collection by working with the Region and securing a private collection company. Councillor Dasko commended the building design and stated that the project reflected the intent of the Mayor’s Affordable Housing Task Force. Councillor Dasko thanked the community and planning staff for their contributions.
Mayor Parrish expressed appreciation to Councillor Dasko, describing the project as an excellent example of affordable housing and thanked the developer and City staff for their efforts.
