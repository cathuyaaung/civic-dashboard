8.
PUBLIC QUESTION PERIOD - 15 Minute Limit
Zofia Beaver, Resident provided the following written submission:
Why are basement apartments not taxed at a higher rate due to the fact that they use double the services? Practically every household has tenants and they should be taxed more, please explain.
Marisa Chiu, Commissioner, Corporate Services, Chief Financial Officer and Treasurer responded with information related to property assessment value and different classifications, and factors contributing to the property tax.
Mike Fisher, Resident provided the following written submission:
What expenditures can be reduced, delayed and/or omitted completely or until the Canadian economy recovers ? Residents do this all the time when they consider groceries, going out for dinner, major purchases like automobiles /family vacations so the same mindset should apply here too.
Given the fact that employment, wages, pensions and other income sources are not a given or growing at the proposed budget percentages; how can City Council ( who are also Regional Council members participating in that increase calculation) reasonably expect the taxpayers to absorb another significant tax increase?
Direction was provided to Finance staff to provide a written response to M. Fisher's questions.
Freeda Ricardo, Resident asked the following questions:
Why is taxpayer money being directed into services that are ineffective, poorly managed, and disconnected from real community needs, instead of into areas like police response times, reliable online reporting, and essential services where the public can actually see results?
With the rapid rise in high-rise development, existing taxpayers are already paying for the infrastructure needed to support these projects. These developments primarily benefit the builders, not the residents funding the city. Why are developers not covering these costs themselves and why are taxpayers being asked to pay for growth that does not benefit us?
In response, Raj Sheth, Commissioner, Community Services outlined the process of planning with multiple groups and the approval process is up to Council.
Councillor Reid spoke to the benefits of the Safe City Mississauga and Business Watch programs and impact to the safety ecosystem.
Marisa Chiu, Commissioner, Corporate Services, Chief Financial Officer and Treasurer provided information on housing assessment growth impacts and benefits, development charges, legislation reforms and impact, and advocacy to fund housing infrastructure.
Frank Mazzolin, Resident asked the following questions:
Why is the Region of Peel’s police per capita amount higher than Toronto’s?
Why is the City paying for the hospital when it is the Province’s responsibility
Mayor Parrish responded with information on the history of the Region’s establishment and the value of buildings and spoke to advocacy efforts with the province to change the formula; and further spoke to the payment custom regarding the hospital expansion.
