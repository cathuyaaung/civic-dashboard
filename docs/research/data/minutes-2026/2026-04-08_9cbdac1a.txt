7.3
Asra Tauseef, Student and Maymunah Qazi, Student with respect to the cost barrier to Recreation
Asra Tauseef and Maymunah Qazi raised concerns about high recreation program fees limiting resident participation and requested improved accessibility through expanded financial assistance, free program hours, and fee reductions.
In response to Councillor Tedjo's enquiry for clarification of the Active Assist program budget and whether funding levels could be revisited, Lisa Boyce‑Gonsalves, Director Recreation & Culture advised the program is well utilized, not at capacity, and comparable to other municipalities.
In response to Councillor Reid's enquiry on whether barriers are related to program availability or space, A. Tauseef confirmed it was program availability and suggested awareness included social media, billboards, and flyers.
Councillor Fonseca discussed age limitations in existing programs, including Swim to Survive, and asked staff to explore expanding eligibility and free access to aquatics.
Mayor Parrish highlighted a school board credit program, directed staff to review the costs of additional low‑ or no‑cost programs, and encouraged outreach to local MPPs.

7.4
Item 10.1 Lorraine Johnson, Naturalization Bylaw Reform Advocate
Lorraine Johnson spoke to her work and advocacy on naturalized landscapes, noted her consultation on the report, commended staff for its thoroughness, and urged Council to adopt the recommendations.
In response to Councillor Damerla's enquiry regarding allowing species such as dog‑strangling vine, L. Johnson explained the nuisance weed list is limited, largely applies to agricultural contexts, and was not intended to regulate urban naturalized areas; expanding it would complicate enforcement. Amory Ngan, Manager, Forestry confirmed animal and pet health considerations were reviewed.
In response to Councillor Horneck's expressed concerns about distinguishing naturalized from neglected gardens, L. Johnson suggested enhanced public education, clearer guidance through the City website, partnerships with conservation authorities, and a process for repeat complaints.

7.5
Item 10.1 Karen Barnes, Subject Matter Expert
1. K Barnes Presentation re 10.1.pdf
Karen Barnes provided an overview of how municipalities across Canada are embracing urban meadows, rewilding, and naturalization. She noted that a 20‑cm height restriction would hinder transitions from traditional lawns to meadow landscapes and stated that allergy concerns are more closely linked to lawnmower equipment and air pollution than to grass itself.
In response to Councillor Damerla's enquiry regarding invasive species and their ecological impacts, Ms. Barnes provided expert commentary on the role and function of certain plant species.

16.1
A proposed or pending acquisition or disposition of land by the municipality or local board:
Public Comments:
Authorization to Approve and Execute a License Extension Agreement with The Regional Municipality of Peel (Ward 9)
Raj Sheth, Commissioner of Community Services, and Olimpia Start, Manager, Realty Services. spoke to the subject matter and answered questions from Members of Committee.
RECOMMENDATION GC-0216-2026
Moved By Councillor C. Fonseca
That the Commissioner of Corporate Services, Chief Financial Officer and Treasurer and the City Clerk, be authorized to execute a License Extension Agreement, including all ancillary documents and any subsequent amending or extension agreements, between the City of Mississauga, (the “City”) as licensor and the Regional Municipality of Peel (the “Region”) as licensee with respect to the certain lands in Ward 9 on the terms detailed in this Closed Session Corporate Report dated March 9, 2026 from the Commissioner of Corporate Services, Chief Financial Officer and Treasurer and in a form satisfactory to the City Solicitor or their designate.
That all necessary by-laws be enacted.
YES (7)
Councillor S. Dasko, Councillor C. Fonseca, Councillor S. McFadden, Councillor A.Tedjo, Councillor M. Reid, Councillor B. Butt, and Councillor N. Hart
ABSENT (4)
Councillor J. Kovac, Mayor Parrish, Councillor D. Damerla, and Councillor J. Horneck
Carried (7 to 0)
16.2
Advice that is subject to solicitor-client privilege, including communications necessary for that purpose:
Public Comments:
Settlement regarding City of Mississauga v. PSCC No. 1136 et al (Ridgeway Plaza)
Graham Walsh, Commissioner of Legislative Services, Domenic Tudino, Director, Special Projects, Legal Services, Nupur Kotecha, Legal Counsel Municipal Law, and Chris Rouse, Director, Development & Design spoke to the subject matter and answered questions from Members of Committee.
RECOMMENDATION GC-0217-2026
Moved By Councillor S. McFadden
That the City Solicitor and Commissioner of Legislative Services or designate be authorized to execute the Minutes of Settlement between The Corporation of the City of Mississauga and Peel Standard Condominium Corporations 1136 and 1140 (Ridgeway Plaza) in respect of the court proceedings known as City of Mississauga v. PSCC No. 1136 et al , No. CV-25-00004269-0000, attached as Appendix 1 to the Corporate Report from the City Solicitor and Commissioner of Legislative Services, dated April 6, 2026, entitled “Settlement regarding City of Mississauga v. PSCC No. 1136 et al (Ridgeway Plaza)”, along with any ancillary documents or amending agreements.
That the City Solicitor and Commissioner of Legislative Services or designate, be authorized to take such steps as are necessary to complete, implement, and pursue enforcement measures in connection with the Minutes of Settlement, with the assistance of such City of Mississauga staff and external counsel as may be appropriate.
YES (8)
Councillor S. Dasko, Councillor C. Fonseca, Councillor S. McFadden, Councillor A.Tedjo, Councillor J. Horneck, Councillor M. Reid, Councillor B. Butt, and Councillor N. Hart
ABSENT (3)
Councillor J. Kovac, Mayor Parrish, and Councillor D. Damerla
Carried (8 to 0)
