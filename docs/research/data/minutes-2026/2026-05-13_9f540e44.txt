15.1
A Notice of Motion regarding the City Of Mississauga to endorse the Fossil Fuel Non-Proliferation Treaty (Councillor Fonseca)
1. Fossil Fuel Treaty Motion Final_.pdf
The discussion and vote took place under item 7.11.
RESOLUTION 0098-2026
Moved By Councillor C. Fonseca
Seconded By Deputy Mayor and Councillor M. Mahoney
WHEREAS, the scientific consensus is clear that human activities are primarily responsible for accelerating global climate change, and that the climate crisis now represents one of the preeminent threats to global civilization; and
WHEREAS, the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) reported in 2018 that we must achieve net zero in greenhouse gas emissions (GHGs) by the middle of this century in order to have a reasonable chance of limiting global warming to 1.5 degrees Celsius; and
WHEREAS, the IPCC released its Sixth Assessment Report from Working Group II, which was approved by 195 member states, in February 2022, which notes that there is high confidence that “the rise in weather and climate extremes has led to some irreversible impacts as natural and human systems are pushed beyond their ability to adapt.”; and
WHEREAS, the IPPC’s final report was released in March 2023 and updated previous projections with the latest climate science, stating that based on current consumption and future projections the world will not meet its 1.5°C warming target, meriting immediate and aggressive climate action to limit the irreversible effects of climate change; and
WHEREAS, the City of Mississauga is already experiencing the effects of climate change with hotter/drier summers, more frequent flooding events and increased exposure to wildfire smoke; and
WHEREAS, our community is being impacted by the health and safety risks associated with the use of fossil fuels, particularly those who also face socioeconomic and health inequities, including low-income families, those experiencing homelessness, People of Colour and Indigenous people, youth, seniors, those experiencing mental and physical disabilities, and people with health conditions; and
WHEREAS, it is necessary to bring perspectives of youth into policy discussions relating to climate change, as they are the ones most critically affected by the negative impacts of climate change; and
WHEREAS, the economic opportunities presented by a clean energy transition far outweigh the opportunities presented by an economy supported by expanding fossil fuel use and extraction; and
WHEREAS, on June 24, 2019 the City of Mississauga joined the growing number of Canadian municipalities declaring a Climate Emergency, stressing the need for immediate action to address extreme weather events caused by climate change and to then reduce greenhouse gas emissions; and
WHEREAS, in December 2019, City Council adopted the City’s first comprehensive Climate Change Action Plan (CCAP), highlighting the need for green building standards, resilient and green infrastructure, accelerating climate innovation, and the need for low emissions mobility to meet our 2050 climate goals; and
WHEREAS, in December 2019, City Council adopted the Corporate Green Building Standard (CGBS), which is a set of environmental performance requirements, including reduction of greenhouse gases and energy efficiency requirements that apply to major renovations and new construction of City owned facilities.
The Standard sets three increasing levels of performance that design teams can elect to pursue according to a specific project’s characteristics and constraints; and
WHEREAS, the City’s CCAP identifies key actions to reduce greenhouse gas emissions and increase the City’s resilience to climate impacts. This includes a commitment to improve energy efficiency, install renewable energy and advance low carbon district energy systems to heat and cool buildings; and
WHEREAS, Fire Station 125 was designed with an environmentally responsible approach, achieves net zero energy, and meets the City’s CGBS. Other projects that have or are intending to meet the City’s CGBS include the Burnhamthorpe Community Centre, Carmen Corbasson Community Centre renovation, Fire station 124, and the South Common Community Centre; and
WHEREAS, on March 3, 2021, City Council endorsed a motion calling on the Government of Ontario to phase out its gas-fired power plants to reduce greenhouse gas emissions (GHG) to ensure that Ontario meets its climate targets; and
WHEREAS, on March 27, 2023, City Council endorsed a motion to reaffirm and strengthen the City’s commitment to climate action. The City will examine new interim 2030 targets to help reach net zero by or before 2050; and
WHEREAS, a global initiative has been underway calling for a Fossil Fuel Non-Proliferation Treaty that would end new fossil fuel exploration and expansion, phase out existing production in line with the global commitment to limit warming to 1.5°C, and accelerate equitable transition plans; and
WHEREAS, the Fossil Fuel Non-Proliferation Treaty has been formally called for by 18 nation states: Vanuatu and Tuvalu in 2022; Tonga, Fiji, Solomons, Niue, East Timor, Antigua & Barbuda, Palau, Colombia, Samoa, and Nauru in 2023; Marshall Islands, The Bahamas, and Pakistan in 2024; and Cambodia in 2025; in addition to public endorsements from the World Health Organization, the European Parliament, the Vatican, and over 140 cities and subnational governments globally including several Canadian cities: Vancouver (first globally to sign), Toronto, Montreal, and Ottawa; and
WHEREAS, Donna Grantis’ deputation to the Environmental Action Committee regarding the Fossil Fuel Non-Proliferation Treaty was presented on February 3, 2026, where it was received by Committee and thereafter considered at General Committee on February 18, 2026 and subsequently adopted by Council on February 25, 2026; now therefore
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED, the City of Mississauga formally endorses the call for a Fossil Fuel Non-Proliferation Treaty; and
BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, the City of Mississauga urges the Province of Ontario and the Federal Government to support the initiative for a Fossil Fuel Non-Proliferation Treaty; and
BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the City of Mississauga affirms its ongoing commitment to the goals of the Paris Climate Agreement and the GHG reduction targets as called for by the IPCC, and pledges to meet its proportionate GHG reductions under the Paris Climate Agreement; and
BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the City of Mississauga again call on the Province of Ontario to boldly develop province-wide Green Development Standards, giving the development community the roadmap for sustainability, or provide municipalities the tools to enforce these standards themselves.
YES (12)

15.2
Revised: A Notice of Motion regarding an Enbridge Above Ground Valve Compound and Traffic Safety (Councillor Damerla)
1. Rev Motion AGVC - May 11.pdf
The discussion and vote took place under item 7.10.
RESOLUTION 0099-2026
Moved By Councillor D. Damerla
Seconded By Deputy Mayor and Councillor M. Mahoney
Whereas Trans-Northern Pipelines Inc. (referred to as “TNPI”) has constructed an above ground valve compound (referred to as “AGVC”) containing high pressure jet fuel petroleum products for delivery to Ontario and Quebec,
And Whereas the AGVC was originally intended to be installed underground but was relocated above ground because of TNPI internal corporate requirements;
And Whereas the AGVC is located immediately at the Dickson Road and Premium Way intersection which is the sole access to over 70 homes (known as Dickson Park) and a busy Catholic Cemetery;
And Whereas an AGVC emergency incident at this intersection will deny street access for all Dickson Park residents to all fire trucks, police vehicles, ambulances and other emergency vehicles;
And Whereas a multiuse trail (pedestrians, bicycles and scooters) has been constructed along Premium Way, separating the road (Premium Way) and the AGVC;
And Whereas the location and design of the AGVC has resulted in sightlines that are unsafe and unacceptable for local residents, road users and pedestrians;
And Whereas TNPI has indicated that the Province of Ontario (Ministry of Transportation Ontario) has the authority to direct changes and/or the relocation of the valve compound;
And Whereas TNPI has acknowledged willingness to move the above ground valve compound if instructed to do so by the Government of Ontario;
And Whereas the City of Mississauga prioritizes neighbourhood and road safety and visibility standards for all public rights-of-way;
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED,:
1.The City of Mississauga requests that the Province of Ontario direct TNPI to relocate the above
ground valve compound to improve neighbourhood safety and sightlines for multiuse trail users and all motorists and
2. The Mayor be authorized to write to the Province of Ontario, with copies sent to TNPI, local MPP’s and MP’s outlining Council’s and residents’ concerns regarding traffic safety and visibility impacts associated with the current placement of the compound.
YES (12)
Councillor S. Dasko, Councillor C. Fonseca, Deputy Mayor and Councillor J. Kovac , Mayor C. Parrish, Councillor D. Damerla, Deputy Mayor and Councillor M. Mahoney, Councillor S. McFadden, Councillor A. Tedjo, Councillor J. Horneck, Councillor M. Reid, Councillor B. Butt, and Councillor N. Hart
