2. How will the City ensure, through the new municipal framework, that hyperscale data centres are prohibited in E1 and E2 zones, office and commercial zones, and any locations in close proximity to residential neighbourhoods, and ensure these are directed exclusively to appropriate industrial areas (E3) aligned with their scale, noise profile, and infrastructure demands?

impacts of salt pollution;

contractor training and certification, and a single set of provincially endorsed standard best management practices for snow and ice management; and
THAT CVC in partnership with the CTC Source Protection Committee develop public messaging that supports this resolution; and further

A Notice of Motion regarding expanding cooling requirements and cooling amenity spaces in apartment buildings (Councillor Damerla)
1. Motion to Expand Cooling Requirements and Cooling Amenity Spaces in Apartment Buildings.pdf
The discussion and vote took place under item 16.2.
RESOLUTION 0162-2026
Moved By Councillor D. Damerla
Seconded By Councillor M. Reid
WHEREAS the City of Mississauga’s Adequate Temperature By-law 0110-2018 establishes requirements for minimum indoor temperatures and includes provisions respecting “adequate and suitable cooling” where air conditioning is provided; and
WHEREAS these cooling provisions apply only in circumstances where air conditioning is already available and do not require landlords to provide cooling in all rental units; and
WHEREAS extreme heat events are increasing in frequency and intensity, posing significant health risks, particularly to vulnerable populations; and
WHEREAS many apartment buildings contain shared indoor amenity spaces that could serve as accessible cooling areas during periods of extreme heat; and
WHEREAS other municipalities, including the City of Toronto, have adopted maximum indoor temperature standards and cooling space requirements;
NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED THAT
1. Staff be directed to review the City’s Adequate Temperature By-law 0110-2018 and report back on potential amendments to expand existing cooling provisions, including:
(a) broadening the application of “adequate and suitable cooling,” currently defined as a maximum indoor temperature of 26 degrees Celsius (26°C), and considering options to improve protection for tenants during periods of extreme heat and maintain safe indoor temperatures in rental residential buildings, including but not limited to indoor temperature standards, access to cooling devices, building retrofit and design measures, operational requirements, and other heat-mitigation approaches; and
(b) establishing requirements for Cooling Amenity Spaces within apartment buildings;
2. Staff consider requirements for apartment buildings with shared amenity areas to:
(a) designate at least one indoor common area as a Cooling Amenity Space;
(b) maintain such space at or below 26 degrees Celsius (26°C) during the cooling season or during declared heat events;
(c) ensure the space is accessible to residents, including extended hours during heat warnings; and
(d) provide clear notice to residents regarding the location and availability of such spaces;
3. Staff consult with Peel Public Health and housing, tenant representatives, building owners, and accessibility advisory bodies as part of this review;
4. Staff report back to Council with recommendations, including draft by-law amendments and any financial or operational implications.

1. Data Centre Motion - MR 2026 July 14 2026 _FINAL_(1505223.1).pdf
The discussion and vote took place under item 8.26.
RESOLUTION 0161-2026
Moved By Councillor M. Reid
Seconded By Councillor S. McFadden
WHEREAS Mississauga has long been recognized as one of Canada's leading economic engines, attracting investment, innovation, employment and advanced infrastructure;
AND WHEREAS data centres, cloud computing facilities, artificial intelligence infrastructure and similar facilities are becoming critical components of Canada's economic competitiveness, technological sovereignty, cybersecurity and national security;
AND WHEREAS the Government of Canada has identified sovereign computing capacity, domestic digital infrastructure, workforce development and responsible AI development as national priorities;
AND WHEREAS municipalities have an obligation to ensure that emerging forms of development occur in a manner that protects public infrastructure, environmental sustainability, neighbourhood compatibility and long-term fiscal responsibility;
AND WHEREAS the Province of Ontario has not established a comprehensive planning or regulatory framework specific to artificial intelligence data centres and other large-scale digital infrastructure facilities;
AND WHEREAS residents have expressed legitimate concerns regarding electricity demand and grid capacity, water consumption, wastewater servicing, noise, greenhouse gas emissions, transportation, infrastructure, emergency management, employment generation, municipal financial impacts, and community benefit;
AND WHEREAS the Planning Act permits municipalities to enact an Interim Control By-law while undertaking studies respecting land use planning matters;
AND WHEREAS Council recognizes the economic opportunities associated with digital infrastructure while recognizing the need for a comprehensive municipal framework before permitting additional large-scale facilities.
THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED
1. That staff undertake a comprehensive review of the City's planning policies, zoning regulations and development approval framework, for major digital infrastructure facilities, including data centres (artificial intelligence, hyperscale, enterprise, cloud computing, and comparable uses).
2. That staff prepare an Interim Control By-law pursuant to section 38 of the Planning Act for Council's consideration to temporarily prohibit the establishment of major digital infrastructure facilities throughout Mississauga for up to one (1) year while a planning study is undertaken, and that staff report back on the need for any extension, up to a maximum of one additional year as permitted by section 38 of the Planning Act, if additional time is required to complete the study and bring forward recommendations.
3. That the review include Official Plan policies and designations Zoning By-law permissions, site plan standards, infrastructure, servicing, environmental, economic, employment, taxation, public health, climate, and national security considerations.
4. That staff consult utilities, Peel Region, Indigenous Communities, industry, environmental experts, academic institutions, residents and community organizations.
5. That staff consider the feasibility for voluntary community benefits (e.g. parks, recreation, public art) in connection with data centre developments and report back to Council with findings and potential implementation options.
6. That staff evaluate legislative tools to regulate operational impacts including noise, water, energy, emissions, heat generation and administrative penalties where appropriate.
7. That staff report on opportunities for public reporting respecting energy, water, environmental performance and community benefits where legally permissible.
8. That staff undertake comprehensive public engagement.
9. That staff provide an interim report setting out the proposed study scope and consultation strategy, based on current data and existing processes, and presenting Council with options to refine or adjust the scope of the study.
10. That staff return with recommendations for implementation, such as Official Plan and Zoning By-law amendments, site plan guidelines, etc. ,
11. That copies of this resolution be forwarded to AMO, FCM, the Province of Ontario and the Government of Canada requesting a coordinated regulatory framework.
12. That the Interim Control By-law be presented at the September 16, 2026 Council meeting.
