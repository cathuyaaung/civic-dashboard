DEPUTATIONS
8.1
Item 11.1 Brian Bentz, President and CEO of Alectra Inc.
1. Item 8.1 Revised Mississauga Deputation June 24 2026 (Public) Final2-BB-June 22_2026.pdf
B. Bentz spoke to the 2025 Consolidated MIFRA results, including affordability and reliability, operating costs to customers, and capital renewal projects.
Members of Council enquired about the development of large data centres within the City of Mississauga, including funding sources, implications under the Distribution System Code, and the socialization of rate-related costs.
B. Bentz responded to questions, advising that capital costs are generally borne by the beneficiary and provided additional clarification regarding the scale and impact of large distribution system costs.
Item 11.1 was brought up and voted on.
8.2
James Wang, Peel Region Science Fair Regional Coordinator regarding the Peel Region Science Fair and to discuss the Canada Wide Science Fair
1. Item 8.2 Overview of PRSF 2026.pdf
J. Wang and Marc De Benedetti provided an overview of the Peel Region Science Fair, including its three primary programs offered to students, such as student mentorship initiatives and the Team Peel participants attending the Canada-Wide Science Fair.
Aarsh Patel, Maanya Shah, Ananyaa T, Navya Madken, Zunaira Wasi, Madhav Saravanan expressed appreciation to the City of Mississauga for its funding support and spoke to the positive impact the Peel Region Science Fair has had on them personally.
Members of Council congratulated the Peel Region Science Fair and discussed efforts to recruit new students to the program, as well as opportunities to apply for City of Mississauga grants and to the Growing Healthy Cities program. J. Wang and M. De Benedetti responded to questions.
Raj Sheth, Commissioner of Community Services, and Andrew Whittemore, Commissioner of Planning and Building, also responded and provided additional information regarding the Innovation Grant.
8.3
Gregory J. Stulen, Resident regarding fairness for electric vehicle drivers
1. Item 8.3 Deck - EV Charging Fairness - City of Mississauga.pdf
G. Stulen addressed Council regarding residents living in multi-unit dwellings and proposed the creation of an electric vehicle (EV) access pass to allow drivers to use EV charging stations located in public parking facilities.
Councillors Horneck and Kovac enquired about the number of EV charging locations within the City of Mississauga. Raj Sheth, Commissioner of Community Services, and Nadia Paladino, Director, Parks, Forestry and Environment, responded by referencing a previously approved Council report on EV charging infrastructure and providing information regarding pricing. Direction was given to staff to meet with G. Stulen offline to further discuss potential EV charging options.
8.4
Item 11.2 Christopher Valeri, Project Director, Strategic City Building Initiatives
1. Item 8.4 2026.06.24 LAC Vision - Approach for public consultation.pdf
C. Valeri provided an overview of the Living Arts Centre Redevelopment Vision, including the proposed approach to public consultation, project funding, timeline, and endorsement considerations.
Members of Council raised concerns regarding proceeding with public consultation prior to completion of a business case, funding for consultation and public engagement, the scope of both the consultation and the project, and the application of the Municipal Accommodation Tax. Members of Council also spoke to investing in the downtown core, the need for transparency, potential impacts on other City infrastructure projects, the importance of identifying return on investment to taxpayers, the role of Visit Mississauga, the need for additional hotel capacity, and previous provincial funding mechanisms.
Mayor Parrish responded to concerns raised, including the use of convention centres and the importance of future investments in the City of Mississauga.
C. Valeri, Andrew Whittemore, Commissioner of Planning and Building, Victoria Clarke, Director, Tourism, Geoff Wright, City Manager, and Marisa Chiu, Commissioner of Corporate Services, responded to questions, providing clarification on the project scope, public engagement approach, City infrastructure considerations, tourism-related funding, and the capital budget process.
Councillor McFadden arrived at 11:44AM. Mayor Parrish left the meeting at 11:45AM and Councillor Mahoney resumed the role of Chair.
8.5
Item 11.2 George Tavares, Resident
G. Tavares spoke to the proposed Downtown Music and Convention Centre, including its role in building a vibrant downtown core, its potential to support housing development in the City of Mississauga, the availability of key public documents, and the estimated cost of the project. Andrew Whittemore, Commissioner of Planning and Building, spoke to the housing growth forecast.
Councillors Mahoney and Fonseca spoke to funding opportunities and the inclusion of federal and provincial housing-enabling funding in the public consultation process.
A. Whittemore, and Marisa Chiu, Commissioner of Corporate Services, responded to questions.
8.6
Item 11.2 Rahul Mehta, Founder, Sustainable Mississauga
R. Mehta spoke to the Living Arts Centre Redevelopment Vision and the proposed approach for public consultation. He raised concerns regarding the timing of the process in relation to the upcoming Municipal Election, emphasized the need for greater resident input, and suggested that consideration of the project be deferred until after the Municipal Election.
8.6.1
Item 11.4 John Dunlop, Manager, Indigenous Relations, Culture and Heritage Planning
1. Council Presentation-Creative Advantage_24062026_2 (1).pdf
J. Dunlop spoke to the Mayor’s Arts and Museums Task Force Report and outlined The Creative Advantage, which consolidates the Task Force’s recommendations with the City’s cultural strategy and policy work undertaken over the past decade.
Councillor Dasko enquired about public consultation. J. Dunlop responded by outlining the ongoing consultation activities currently being undertaken.
8.7
Item 11.4 Mike Douglas, Executive Director, Mississauga Arts Council
1. Item 8.7 June 24 Deputation Arts Org v3.pdf
Nasrin Gilbert, Visual Arts Mississauga; Anna Gulbinski, Art Gallery of Mississauga (AGM); Margot Stevens, CreativeHub 1352; Claudine LaRochelle, Mississauga Symphony Orchestra; and Mike Douglas, Mississauga Arts Council spoke to the Mayor’s Arts and Museums Task Force Report. The speakers highlighted opportunities to better align City policies and funding structures with long-term sustainability goals, support the development of a sustainable creative economy, and protect and create cultural spaces within Mississauga.
Members of Council enquired whether the perspectives of arts organizations had been adequately represented through the Task Force process, the coordination of the Task Force’s recommendations, and opportunities to advocate to other orders of government for additional funding support.
Items 11.4 and 16.1 were moved up and voted on.
Councillors Dasko and Kovac spoke in support of the Mayor’s Arts and Museums Task Force Report.
8.8
Item 15.2.4 Julie Desjardins, Resident
1. Item 8.8 Council June 24 PPT.pdf

Desjardin also requested that a written response from Planning staff be provided to Council prior to the submission of a final recommendation.

Item 18.1 Athina Tagidou on behalf of the Applewood Hills & Heights Residents’ Association (AHHRA’s)
1. Item 8.9 AHHRA - Council June 24th 2026 Agenda Item 18.1 Riverspray Cres. by Athina Tagidou.pdf
A. Tagidou spoke to the proposed by-law to amend Traffic By-law 0555-2000, as amended, regarding Extended Parking, No Parking, and Lower Driveway Boulevard Parking (LDBP) regulations for locations in Ward 3, and requested that Riverspray Crescent be excluded from the proposed by-law.
Councillors Fonseca and Horneck enquired about the definition of Lower Driveway Boulevard Parking (LDBP), the application and request process for LDBP, whether a petition had been submitted for Riverspray Crescent, and the effectiveness of the LDBP program.

16.2
A Notice of Motion regarding a provincial review of benchmarking, funding formulas, and length-of-stay standards for emergency shelters serving women and children experiencing intimate partner violence (Councillor Hart)
1. Intimate Partner Violence Mississauga.pdf
Members of Council spoke in support of the Notice of Motion and highlighted concerns regarding inadequate funding formulas to support individuals experiencing intimate partner violence.
RESOLUTION 0147-2026
Moved By Councillor C. Fonseca
Seconded By Councillor N. Hart
WHEREAS women and children experiencing intimate partner violence rely on emergency shelters as a critical component of their safety, recovery, and transition to stable housing;
WHEREAS adequate lengths of stay in emergency shelters are often necessary to support safety planning, access to social services, legal supports, mental health resources, and housing stabilization;
WHEREAS funding formulas and service benchmarks directly influence shelter capacity, operations, and the ability of service providers to meet community needs;
Whereas The City of Mississauga declared Intimate Partner Violence an epidemic on September 13 th 2023;
WHEREAS municipalities across Ontario utilize varying approaches to shelter funding and length-of-stay expectations, providing opportunities for benchmarking and best-practice review;
WHEREAS any reductions to funding based on the formulas may have unintended consequences on service levels and the availability of supports for individuals fleeing intimate partner violence;
THEREFORE BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED THAT the Mayor, on behalf of Council, write to the Premier of Ontario, the Minister of Children, Community and Social Services, the Minister of Municipal Affairs and Housing, and the local Members of Provincial Parliament requesting a provincial review of benchmarking, funding formulas, and length-of-stay standards for emergency shelters serving women and children experiencing intimate partner violence, with a view to ensuring adequate shelter stays and preventing reductions in funding that could negatively impact service delivery and client outcomes;
