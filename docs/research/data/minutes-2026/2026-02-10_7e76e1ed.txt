8.
PUBLIC QUESTION PERIOD - 15 Minute Limit
Glenn Voakes, Resident submitted the following two questions related to item 11.1:
Question 1: At the Dec 3 GC meeting Matthew Sweet responded to Councillor Mahoney’s question “who enforces e-scooters?”. Matthew responded to the effect moving e-scooters was a police matter and idle e-scooters was Mississauga bylaw enforcement. Uncertain of the response post the meeting I reached out to Claudia D’Amico of the PRP for clarification. Claudia states any infraction (speeding, safety etc.) unless on the actual road is not a police matter. It is Mississauga bylaw.
Can this discrepancy in enforcement be clarified and communicated to all appropriate parties including the mayor and all council members?
In response, Matthew Sweet, Manager, Active Transportation noted that police officers have the authority to enforce violations on moving e-scooters (or other devices in motion) and that enforcement applies across roadways, sidewalks, and multi-use trails within the right-of-way.
Question 2: At the September 2025 Road Safety Committee (RSC) meeting I addressed safety concerns centred around lack of helmets. I asked the RSC chair to discuss the matter at the next meeting and clarify and set the record straight. This did not happen apparently investigation is still under way. I would like to draw you attention to the photo included in the presentation where 5 of 7 e-scooters, 71.5%, do not even have helmets available when that apparently is a contractual requirement.
This evening through the chair I ask for discussion to take place among the MCAC members if they firmly believe not wearing a helmet presents risk for serious injury or not and which committee, the RSC or the MCAC, owns this issue because they effectively both cannot?
Councillor Tedjo, Chair requested that staff speak to the issue of enforcement regarding helmet wearing.
In response, M. Sweet spoke to requirements and provincial regulations regarding helmet wearing and noted the City’s limitation on creating stricter rules. Further, M. Sweet spoke to the contractual agreement for e-scooters through the Shared Micromobility Program and continued monitoring and collaboration with the service providers.
Councillor Tedjo, Chair spoke to having an offline discussion around the matter, and promoting helmet use behavior and to see if it can be added to the Committee’s work plan.
Councillor Fonseca spoke to ongoing discussions between Road Safety Committee and Mississauga Cycling Advisory Committee on safety communications and supported the Chair’s suggestion to take the discussion offline to clarify each committee’s mandate.
Moaz Ahmad, Citizen Member spoke to the injury data presented at the Road Safety Committee and noted that the data is in the process of being cleaned. M. Ahmad supported the suggestion to discuss helmet messaging offline for clearer communication through both committees.
Councillor Fonseca spoke to the Mississauga Cycling Advisory Committee lacking police representation, noted limited by-law officer capacity for enforcement, and not having all relevant parties included creates confusion and frustration for the public. Councillor Fonseca suggested exploring opportunities for the police to attend Committee meetings, particularly on relevant topics.
Councillor Tedjo, Chair spoke to the CIRT team presenting to Committee in the past and the topic around including the police would be a worthwhile topic to discuss.

INFORMATION ITEMS
11.1
Shared Micro-mobility Program – 2025 Season Update
1. Micro-mobility Program Update - 0532-2025.pdf
2. Appendix 1 - Parking Station Examples.pdf
3. Appendix 2 - Monitoring and Evaluation 2025.pdf
4. Appendix 3 - Educational Materials.pdf
5. Appendix 4 - E-scooter Safety Flyer.pdf
This item was considered at General Committee on December 3, 2025 (GC-0577-2025) and subsequently adopted at Council on December 10, 2025 and is shared for information only.
Matthew Sweet, Manager, Active Transportation spoke to the update related to the Shared Micro-mobility program for 2025, outlining comments on ridership, program performance issues raised, personal scooter use and challenges, and plans moving forward.
Councillor Tedjo, Chair spoke to concerns raised at Council regarding the shared micro-mobility program and micro-mobility generally; and further highlighted that residents may not distinguish between the program scooters and personal scooters/devices, with frustration centered on personal devices that lack speed limits and geo-fencing—noting the issues are being conflated with the program itself and pose a risk to the program’s continuation.
Moaz Ahmad, Citizen Member spoke to upcoming developments on the e-scooter policy by the Ministry of Transportation (MTO), guidance and recommendations for municipalities regarding e-bike safety by the Association of Municipalities of Ontario (AMO), and legislative updates regarding designations for power-assisted bicycles.
M. Sweet spoke to the provincial e-scooter pilot program, MTO requests and annual data from the City regarding the pilot, AMO e-bike guidance, and spoke to the City’s strategy using educational materials on proper e-scooter use and additional information for residents.
Councillor Tedjo, Chair asked if any changes to the shared micro-mobility program were expected based on year 1 and 2 feedback. In response, M. Sweet spoke to the program evolution, noting areas such as the expansion of physical parking locations and physical demarcations, and noted no further changes were anticipated.
RECOMMENDATION MCAC-0008-2026
Moved By Councillor C. Fonseca
That the Corporate Report entitled, “Shared Micro-mobility Program – 2025 Season Update”, dated November 20, 2025, from the Commissioner of Transportation and Works, be received.
Received
