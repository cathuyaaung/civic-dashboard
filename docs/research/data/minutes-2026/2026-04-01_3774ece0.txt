RESOLUTION 0063-2026
Moved By Councillor M. Reid
Seconded By Councillor S. Dasko
WHEREAS in December, 2020, Council adopted a recommendation to opt-in to the Provincial Electric Kick-style Scooters (“e-scooter”) Pilot Program to permit and regulate the use of e-scooters in the City of Mississauga;
AND WHEREAS the City’s Traffic, Transit and Parks By-laws were amended in February 2021 to permit the use of e-scooters in Mississauga (“the City’s e-scooter permissions”);
AND WHEREAS the City launched its Shared Micro-mobility Program in June 2024 with service providers Bird Canada and Lime Technologies Inc. offering e-bikes and e-scooters for short-term rental;
AND WHEREAS between 2023 and 2025, the Credit Valley Hospital and the Mississauga Hospital recorded 738 e-scooter-related Emergency Department visits, with a year-over-year increase during this period;
AND WHEREAS the most common injuries include fractures, sprains, lacerations and head injuries resulting from falls, collisions, multiple riders on a single scooter, failure to follow traffic signals, or riding on sidewalks where interactions with pedestrians increase risk;
AND WHEREAS many of these injuries appear to be preventable through proper education on the use of e-scooters, the required protective equipment, and the existing rules for safe e-scooter use set out in Provincial regulations and City by-laws; and
AND WHEREAS Peel Regional Police have expressed their own concerns with their ability to effectively enforce the ubiquitous use of e-scooters in Mississauga, including many circumstances where officers are unable to safely pursue riders who fail to comply with traffic laws or municipal by-laws due to the inherent risk such pursuits would pose to the rider, pedestrians, and the public.
Therefore be it resolved that:
1. That staff coordinate with the vendors (Bird and Lime) and Peel Police to collectively establish and enhance their safety measures.
2. That staff work on greater public awareness and education measures related to the use of e-scooters.
YES (11)
Councillor S. Dasko, Councillor C. Fonseca, Deputy Mayor and Councillor J. Kovac , Councillor D. Damerla, Deputy Mayor and Councillor M. Mahoney, Councillor S. McFadden, Councillor A. Tedjo, Councillor J. Horneck, Councillor M. Reid, Councillor B. Butt, and Councillor N. Hart

RESOLUTION 0060-2026
Moved By Councillor B. Butt
Seconded By Councillor S. McFadden
WHEREAS locally elected school board trustees provide essential community representation and ensure accountability within Ontario’s education system;
AND WHEREAS the City of Mississauga relies on dedicated trustee representation to reflect local priorities and advocate for the success of students across our diverse communities;
AND WHEREAS recent provincial supervision of the Peel District School Board and the Dufferin-Peel Catholic District School Board, alongside proposed governance changes, raises concerns regarding the future of local democratic accountability;
AND WHEREAS maintaining direct, elected representation is critical to ensuring that Mississauga’s unique geographic and growth needs are reflected in education decision-making;
THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED THAT:
1. Mississauga Council affirms its strong support for maintaining democratically elected school board trustees for both the Peel District School Board and the Dufferin-Peel Catholic District School Board;
2. Council formally recognizes the vital role of locally elected trustee representation for Mississauga and supports the continuation of meaningful, locally accountable representation;
3. The Province of Ontario be requested to pause any legislative or regulatory changes that would reduce or eliminate elected trustees until a transparent, province-wide consultation with municipalities, school boards, trustees, parents, students, educators and other education stakeholders is completed;
4. The City Clerk forward this resolution to the Premier of Ontario, the Minister of Education, all MPPs representing Mississauga, the Peel District School Board, the Dufferin-Peel Catholic District School Board, all MPPs representing Peel Region, and all Ontario municipalities to encourage collective advocacy for local democratic representation in education.

A Notice of Motion regarding amending the Community Recognition Program Policy (06-04-04) (Mayor Parrish)
1. Flag Raisings Motion.pdf
Councillor Damerla spoke to the lack of public engagement related to the Notice of Motion and requested an extension of time for its approval.
Mayor Parrish addressed the Notice of Motion and referenced the number of groups that have already been confirmed. The Mayor also spoke to issuing a video outlining proof‑of‑identification requirements and the provision of receiving a complimentary Canadian flag.
Robert Trewartha, Director of Strategic Communications and Initiatives, and Graham Walsh, City Solicitor and Commissioner, Legislative Services spoke to the purpose of the Notice of Motion, the evolution of the current Community Recognition Program Policy, and the negative division the issue has caused within the community.
Council voted on the deferral request, and the vote failed.
Councillor Tedjo spoke to allowing groups that are currently scheduled to proceed with their events. G. Walsh responded to questions from Council.
Members of Council further noted that the matter does not affect recognition groups’ ability to hold their own events or to display community group flags.
RESOLUTION 0073-2026
Moved By Mayor C. Parrish
Seconded By Councillor S. Dasko
Whereas the City of Mississauga first introduced the Community Recognition Program Policy in 2016 to raise awareness and encourage participation in fundraising initiatives, public awareness campaigns, cultural, sport and entertainment programs, as well as activities that foster a spirit of community and contribute to enriching the quality of life in Mississauga;
And Whereas this Program initially excluded the raising of national flags on the City’s courtesy flagpole in Celebration Square and the display of national colours on the Civic Centre clock tower;
And Whereas this Program initially began with great success and without controversy while it was limited to the recognition of community-based charities and not-for-profit agencies and initiatives;
And Whereas at the direction of Council, national flags were included in the program in 2017, subject to defined criteria, but have recently become a source of controversy and at times division within the community, and are now taking up significant staff time and incurring legal costs;
And Whereas national flags can continue to be displayed as part of City celebrations such as Carassauga, cultural festivals, and sports gatherings such as FIFA World Cup events.
Therefore Be It Resolved That:
1. With immediate effect, the Community Recognition Program Policy (06-04-04) be amended to once again exclude the raising of national flags on the courtesy flagpole in Celebration Square and the display of lights associated with national colours (with the exception of those of Canada) on the Civic Centre clock tower;
2. The above changes will apply retroactively to any requests that have already been received by City staff as of this date.
3. Staff update the Community Recognition Policy (06-04-04) to reflect these changes prior to June 30, 2026.

A Notice of Motion regarding expense budgets and allowances of Members of Council (Mayor Parrish)
1. MotionCouncilExpenses (1).pdf

RESOLUTION 0074-2026
Moved By Mayor C. Parrish
Seconded By Deputy Mayor and Councillor M. Mahoney
Whereas the expense budgets and allowances of Members of Mississauga City Council have not been comprehensively reviewed during the 2022-2026 term of Council;
And Whereas periodic reviews of Council budgets and expense policies in comparator municipalities support transparency, accountability, and consistency in the use of public funds;
And Whereas municipalities across Ontario employ varying models for the allocation of Council office budgets, the types of expenses eligible to be charged to those budgets, and the hiring, employment, and management of staff working for Members of Council.
Therefore Be It Resolved That:
1. City staff be directed to conduct a benchmarking review of Council office budgets and expense policies in comparator municipalities, including those within the Greater Toronto and Hamilton Area; and
2. That the benchmarking review include, but not be limited to, an examination of:
• the amount and structure of budgets allocated to Members of Council;
• the methodology used to determine budget allocations, including whether budgets are universal or vary by ward;
• the types of expenses that Members of Council are eligible to charge to their office budgets, including any limits, restrictions, or caps;
