2. Appendix 1 - Planning Report.pdf
3. Item 6.1 - Staff Presentation - DARC 25-140 W2 2130 Bromsgrove Road Information Report.pdf
Proposed Clarkson Transit Oriented Community (TOC) to permit seven apartment buildings with heights ranging from 25 to 45 storeys containing 2,434 dwelling units, 2,161 m 2 (23,260 ft 2 ) of non-residential floor area and a new public road
2130 Bromsgrove Road, southwest corner of Bromsgrove and Southdown Roads, north of the Lakeshore West GO rail corridor
Owner: Metrolinx (Applicant: Infrastructure Ontario)
Jennifer Renaud‑Nicholson, Planner, provided an overview of the recommendation report, including the planning process, subject lands, concept plan, applicable Official Plan and Zoning By‑law framework, key issues, and recommendations.
Mary Simpson, Resident, speaking on behalf of the Town of Port Credit Association, acknowledged the need for housing and raised concerns related to land speculation, community revitalization, employment opportunities, cultural vitality, and affordable housing.
Councillor Tedjo expressed concerns regarding deficiencies in the proposal, questioned the absence of Infrastructure Ontario at the meeting and sought clarification on whether the subject lands are surplus lands owned or managed by Infrastructure Ontario. Councillor Tedjo raised further questions regarding the Province’s review of the Major Transit Station Area (MTSA), the scale of development (approximately 2,400 units), the proportion of the site relative to the MTSA, continued development within the floodplain, MiWay service capacity and expansion needs, air quality studies, and next steps for the City. Councillor Tedjo expressed significant concern regarding the approach to housing delivery, including impacts related to air quality, floodplain development, and servicing capacity, and conveyed disappointment with the challenges presented.
Hugh Lynch, Executive Manage, Development South, advised that Infrastructure Ontario does not characterize the initiative as a Minister's Zoning Order (MZO), although that is the mechanism being used, and noted that planning staff are not aware of the relationship between Metrolinx and Infrastructure Ontario. H. Lynch further advised that existing Zoning By‑law provisions would continue to apply and that a Conservation Authority permit would be required for floodplain development H. Lynch noted that air quality is being investigated and that planning staff are awaiting final studies from Infrastructure Ontario.
Luisa Galli, Manager, Executive Manager, Planning Programs, advised that applicable policies have been endorsed and planning staff are awaiting approval of the Official Plan Amendment, and that following passage of the Zoning By‑law the matter would be forwarded to the Province. L. Galli confirmed the site could accommodate approximately 5,000 units.
Alana Tyers, Manager, Transit Facilities, advised that MiWay is experiencing space constraints at its current Clarkson GO operations and noted the need for a larger facility.
Mayor Parrish questioned key issues had this been a standard planning application and expressed concern regarding proposed building heights and density. In response, Hugh Lynch identified concerns related to asset management, transit operations, site selection, density, and infrastructure south of the rail corridor.
Andrew Whittemore, Commissioner, Planning and Building, advised that MTSA-related applications are not appealable and that outcomes would be determined based on Council’s direction through the report.
RECOMMENDATION PDC-0016-2026
Moved By Councillor A. Tedjo
That the Corporate Report dated March 11, 2026 , from the Commissioner of Planning and Building entitled “Proposed Clarkson Transit Oriented Community (TOC) to permit seven apartment buildings with heights ranging from 25 to 45 storeys containing 2,434 dwelling units”, be received for information.
That the minutes of the March 30, 2026, Public Meeting pertaining to the Clarkson Transit Oriented Community (TOC), be forwarded to Infrastructure Ontario for their information and consideration.
That City staff report back to Planning and Development Community upon receiving new information from Infrastructure Ontario.
That 1 (one) oral submission be received.
YES (11)
Councillor S. Dasko, Councillor C. Fonseca, Councillor J. Kovac, Councillor D. Damerla, Councillor M. Mahoney, Councillor S. McFadden, Councillor A. Tedjo, Councillor J. Horneck, Councillor M. Reid, Councillor B. Butt, and Councillor N. Hart
Carried (11 to 0)

Proposed City-Initiated Amendments to Zoning By-law 0225-2007 to permit residential uses (including apartment buildings and townhouses) and commercial uses in a mixed-use format
1725 and 1731 Dundas Street East (presently occupied by Mr. Sub), north side of Dundas Street East, east of Treadwells Drive

J. Bevan, Director, City Planning Strategies, advised that, through the MTSA process, Region of Peel staff were consulted and did not raise any concerns. Some infrastructure-related work will be undertaken to enhance operations. Councillor Fonseca noted that the proposal represents a measured approach that aligns with housing delivery objectives, and thanked City and Regional staff for their continued work.
Mayor Parrish commented that the Region has owned the subject lands for over 20 years and indicated that this represents a timely opportunity for the City of Mississauga.

4. Item 6.5 - Staff Presentation - CIP Update.pdf
Update on the Affordable Rental Community Improvement Plan (CIP)
Caleigh McInnes, Planner, provided an update on Round 2 of the Affordable Rental Housing – Gentle Density Program and outlined next steps.
Councillor Fonseca asked how the announcement would benefit the City of Mississauga, noting that two properties in Ward 3 were receiving funding, and inquired about accessible units and available incentives. A. Whittemore advised that municipalities with development charge relief initiatives would receive priority. A. Whittemore noted that funding would be allocated 40 per cent to housing and 60 per cent to housing-related infrastructure, with ratios subject to change. No details were available regarding accessible units.
Mayor Parrish questioned how existing development charge discounts would affect funding eligibility and suggested that correspondence be sent to Robert Flack, Minister of Municipal Affairs and Housing, prior to finalization of regulations. J. Bevan noted that it would be beneficial if the City could retroactively access the funding. A. Whittemore advised that the City may be treated more favourably and confirmed that planning staff would report back with further details. A. Whittemore agreed with sending correspondence from the Mayor.
Omnia Ibrahim, Resident, spoke to housing affordability challenges faced by youth and students and the broader impacts on families, including subsidy concerns. Mayor Parrish, Councillor Damerla, and Councillor Reid acknowledged the delegation, with questions regarding O. Ibrahim's motivation to make a delegation, housing aspirations, and the connection between affordability, home ownership, and employment opportunities.
O. Ibrahim noted the importance of home ownership and expressed concern about future job prospects given the competitive labour market.
RECOMMENDATION PDC-0020-2026
Moved By Councillor C. Fonseca
1.That City Council support the expansion of funding to the Affordable Rental Housing Community Improvement (CIP) Program from the previously allocated maximum of $44 million to $70 million, with $26 million to be added from the third installment of federal Housing Accelerator Fund (HAF) reserve fund (#35581), to be spent by July 31, 2027, with one million of this updated funding envelope allocated to the Gentle Density Program of the CIP and the rest to the Multi-Residential Program of the CIP.
2. That the report dated March 11, 2026, from the Commissioner of Planning and Building titled “Update on the Affordable Rental Community Improvement Plan (CIP)”, including Round 2 conditional funding recommendations be received for information.
3. That 1 (one) oral submission be received.
YES (11)
Councillor S. Dasko, Councillor C. Fonseca, Councillor J. Kovac, Councillor D. Damerla, Councillor M. Mahoney, Councillor S. McFadden, Councillor A. Tedjo, Councillor J. Horneck, Councillor M. Reid, Councillor B. Butt, and Councillor N. Hart

Proposed City-Initiated Amendments to Zoning By-law 0225-2007
File: BL.09-CIT (All Wards)
Kelsey Martin, Planner, provided an overview of City‑initiated Official Plan and Zoning By‑law Amendments related to day cares, private clubs, the Greenlands Overlay, technical amendments, and staff recommendations.
Jigneshkumar Makwana, Resident, raised concerns regarding increased parking requirements for day care establishments, citing impacts on site functionality, lack of feasibility analysis, policy conflicts related to land dedication, and operational challenges unique to childcare facilities.
J. Makwana requested reconsideration of the proposed parking standards.Councillor Damerla expressed concern that the proposed standards may create additional barriers to childcare access, noting the relationship between childcare and housing affordability. Councillor Damerla questioned whether parking requirements could be based on enrolment and staffing levels rather than floor area, and sought clarification on the issue planning staff were attempting to address. Andrew Whittemore, Commissioner, Planning and Building, acknowledged the challenges raised and noted the intent is to ensure well‑functioning day care facilities while addressing parking impacts in residential neighbourhoods.
Councillor Fonseca sought clarification on the Committee of Adjustment process and possible next steps for the site, highlighting the need to balance accessibility with neighbourhood impacts.
Jordan Lee, Manager, Zoning Initiatives, clarified that the proposed standards would apply to new uses and noted greater flexibility for home‑based child care. J. Lee advised that the Committee of Adjustment is the appropriate avenue to seek relief where warranted.
Councillors Damerla and Mayor Parrish raised concerns regarding parking limitations and the ability to appeal to the Committee of Adjustment if required.
