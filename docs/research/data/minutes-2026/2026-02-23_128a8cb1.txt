3. Item 6.2 - Staff Presentation - OZ-OPA 25-18 W7.pdf
Official Plan Amendment and Rezoning applications to permit a 34 storey apartment building containing 559 dwelling units with ground floor commercial uses and office uses on the second floor
51-55 Dundas Street West and 60, 66, 70 & 78 Agnes Street, north of Dundas Street West, west of Cook Street
Owner: 4 CD’s Inc.
File: OZ/OPA 25-18 W7
Adam Lucas, Planner, provided an overview of the subject lands, including the proposed concept plan, Official Plan Amendment and Zoning By-law Amendment applications, and outstanding matters.
Tony De Franco, Lead Planner, and Morgan Dundas, Sajecki Planning, presented the development proposal, which consists of a 34-storey apartment building. It was noted that the site is located approximately 400 metres from Cooksville GO Station and adjacent to the future Dundas Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) corridor. The surrounding area is evolving, with several developments underway or proposed. The presentation also addressed the proposed retention of the heritage building and ongoing discussions with City planning staff to resolve outstanding issues, including building height and heritage considerations.
Alex Garcia, Resident, expressed concerns regarding parking shortages, traffic impacts, and mobility-related challenges. Additional concerns included potential impacts related to substance abuse activity following demolition of the existing building, building height, snow removal affecting parking availability for customers, and operational challenges for the existing building and its management. A. Garcia also indicated that they are exploring potential for a future development application and expressed concerns that an adjacent vacant lot could create further issues. Councillor Dipika Damerla offered to meet with Alex Garcia and acknowledged homelessness as a broader issue in the area, noting that redevelopment of Cooksville may help address some of these concerns.
Raghav Malhotra, Resident, raised concerns regarding construction impacts, including potential effects on small businesses, shared parking areas, foundational impacts, and overall accountability of planning staff and developers. R. Malhotra emphasized the cultural significance of the Cooksville corridor and noted there is no existing plan to preserve the character amid new developments. Questions were raised regarding measures to support small business survival during construction.
Councillor Damerla thanked R. Malhotra and requested clarification from planning staff regarding construction-related liability. Ashlee Rivet-Boyle, Executive Manager, Development Central, advised that construction management requirements are enforced with developers to encourage best practices, including access planning, noting that the application was at an early stage and that detailed construction matters were premature at this time. Ryan Au, Executive Manager, Development Engineering and Construction, confirmed that any damage to adjacent properties would be the responsibility of the developer and their insurer. Councillor Damerla advised residents to contact the local Business Improvement Area and the Ward 7 office should developers fail to comply with requirements.
Chris Rouse, Director, Development and Design, noted that development applications undergo multiple approval stages and that the process is complex.
RECOMMENDATION PDC-0009-2026
Moved By Councillor D. Damerla
1. That the applications under f ile OZ/OPA 25-18 W7 , 4 CD’s Inc. , 51-55 Dundas Street West and 60, 66, 70 & 78 Agnes Street , to amend the land use designation in Mississauga Official Plan to Residential High Density ; and to change the zoning to RA5-Exception (Apartments – Exception) to permit a 34 storey apartment building containing 559 dwelling units with ground floor commercial uses and office uses on the second floor , be referred back to staff to continue working with the applicant .
2. That the report dated January 7, 2026 from the Commissioner of Planning and Building pertaining to applications under f ile OZ/OPA 25-18 W7, 4 CD’s Inc., 51-55 Dundas Street West and 60, 66, 70 & 78 Agnes Street , is received for information and that Planning and Development Committee deem the statutory public meeting requirements of the Planning Act to have been satisfied.
3. That 3 (three) oral submissions be received.

PUBLIC MEETING INFORMATION REPORT (WARD 7)
1. DARC 25-138 and DARC 25-139 - 0109-2026.pdf
2. Appendix 1.pdf
3. Item 6.3 - Staff Presentation - Infrastructure Ontario.pdf
Proposed Cooksville Transit Oriented Community (TOC) to permit eight apartment buildings on two separate parcels with heights ranging from 28 to 49 storeys containing 3,055 dwelling units, 18 957 m 2 (204,051.45 ft 2 ) of non-residential floor area
3210 Hurontario Street, north of Hillcrest Avenue, west of Hurontario Street
Owner: Metrolinx (Applicant: Infrastructure Ontario)
Andrew Whittemore, Commissioner, Planning and Building, provided background information on the Information Report, noting the Province of Ontario’s investment in Transit in 2022. The intent of the report is to ensure that Council understands the implications of the proposal, and that the approved minutes are required to be forwarded to Infrastructure Ontario.
Ashlee Rivet Boyle, Executive Manager, Development Central, presented an overview of the Transit-Oriented Communities Program, including the process, chronology, concept plans, alignment with the Mississauga Official Plan 2051, Zoning By-law considerations, matters to be addressed, and staff recommendations.
Councillor and Vice-Chair Alvin Tedjo thanked Planning staff and noted that all Transit‑Oriented Communities have been subject to Minister’s Zoning Orders.
Councillor Tedjo expressed appreciation for the process and the public education provided through this exercise.
Councillor Dipika Damerla thanked A. Rivet- Boyle and planning staff for their work, noting that related concerns have been communicated. Councillor Damerla indicated that the outcome of this site could establish a precedent for future developments and emphasized the importance of maintaining quality of life within the broader planning context. Councillor Damerla further requested that the minutes of the meeting be forwarded to the Ministry of Municipal Affairs and Housing.
Naveen Kumar, resident, raised concerns related to adjacent buildings, traffic conditions and the current housing market. In response, Councillor Damerla advised that planning staff have identified sun/shadow and wind study requirements, reiterated support for reduced parking in high-rise developments, and noted concerns regarding continued intensification. Councillor Damerla emphasized the need to balance housing supply with livability considerations. Andrew Whittemore, Commissioner, Planning and Building, advised that the proposal is not a development application and that the Province of Ontario is establishing parameters for how a future developer may develop the lands. A. Whittemore noted that the City of Mississauga is seeking to include requirements where possible, similar to previous Provincial planning tools. A. Whittemore further advised that the Province of Ontario’s intent is to optimize land holdings to support housing delivery, that surface parking areas are anticipated to be redeveloped over time, and that parking provisions are minimal at this stage. A. Whittemore also indicated that future discussions with a developer may address parking, but it is premature to do so at this time. Mr. Kumar was encouraged to connect further with Councillor Damerla.
Alex Ye, resident, expressed support for redevelopment provided growth is undertaken responsibly. Concerns were raised regarding construction impacts, noise, mitigation measures, limited community infrastructure, traffic impacts, and the provision of affordable housing. Councillor Damerla thanked A. Ye and clarified that the proposal is being advanced by the Province of Ontario, not the City of Mississauga. Councillor Damerla also emphasized the need for additional retail opportunities in Cooksville as intensification occurs.
Timothy Kilpert, resident, spoke to the long-term residency in the area and highlighted the emotional impact on residents. Concerns were expressed regarding sunlight impacts, traffic on Hurontario Street, infrastructure capacity, pollution, and congestion. Councillor Damerla thanked T. Kilpert, acknowledged the concerns raised, and reiterated that the lands are owned by the Province of Ontario. Councillor Damerla emphasized the City’s objective of improving walkability in Cooksville and reducing reliance on automobiles and encouraged residents to engage with their provincial representatives.
Maria Heartberg, resident, inquired whether similar developments exist in the City of Mississauga. Ashlee Rivet-Boyle, Executive Manager, Development Central, advised that there are none. M. Heartberg raised concerns regarding walkability, access to amenities, the timing of the project given ongoing LRT construction, and existing issues such as speeding and transit service reliability. M. Heartberg expressed support for land optimization while urging the City to consider infrastructure capacity and community impacts.
RECOMMENDATION PDC-0010-2026
Moved By Councillor D. Damerla
1. That the report dated February 4, 2026 , from the Commissioner of Planning and Building pertaining to the Cooksville Transit Oriented Community (TOC) , Metrolinx , 3210 Hurontario Street , be received for information.
2.That the minutes of the February 23, 2026, Public Meeting pertaining to the Cooksville Transit Oriented Community (TOC), be forwarded to Infrastructure Ontario and to the Ministry of Municipal Affairs and Housing for their information and consideration.
3. That City staff report back to Planning and Development Committee upon receiving new information from Infrastructure Ontario.
4. That 4 (four) oral submissions be received.
YES (11)
Councillor S. Dasko, Councillor C. Fonseca, Councillor J. Kovac, Mayor Parrish, Councillor D. Damerla, Councillor M. Mahoney, Councillor S. McFadden, Councillor J. Horneck, Councillor M. Reid, Councillor B. Butt, and Councillor N. Hart
Carried (11 to 0)

Proposed Amendments to Zoning By-law for Urban Apartment Zones and Amenity Area
File: CD.06-APT (ALL WARDS)
Ameena Khan, Planner, presented an overview of the proposed amendments to the Urban Apartment Zones, including growth and intensification objectives, existing amenity area requirements, additional planning considerations, and next steps.
Councillor and Deputy Mayor John Kovac thanked A. Khan for the presentation and the report and inquired about consultation with the Peel ACORN advocacy group, their method of communication, and the anticipated timeline for the matter to return to Council. In response, A. Khan advised that ACORN expressed a preference for a reduction rather than elimination of amenity space requirements, noting the importance of having space such as a recreation room. A. Khan further advised that the Zoning By-law amendment is anticipated to be brought forward at the end of April and confirmed that ACORN was the only tenant association consulted.
Councillor Chris Fonseca inquired whether other tenant groups were consulted prior to the report and whether there was an opportunity to engage tenants who may be concerned about access to amenities, particularly in Ward 3. Councillor Fonseca highlighted deficiencies in parkland and community space in the Dixie–Dundas area and asked whether there had been any discussion regarding the conversion of Special Policy Area (SPA) lands that have not yet been confirmed into community spaces. In response, Andrew Whittemore, Commissioner, Planning and Building, advised that the report represents a zoning-focused exercise and that a broader strategy regarding community amenities would require further work by Planning staff and additional discussion outside of this process. Councillor Fonseca further inquired about the implications of a reduction in amenity space requirements for new developments. Ameena Khan, Planner, advised that if a developer wishes to apply a rate lower than what is prescribed, an application to the Committee of Adjustment would be required. Jordan Lee, Manager, Zoning Initiatives, clarified that the intent of the proposed rate reduction is to apply only to new buildings, and that planning staff would be required to review and assess the justification for any proposed reduction.
Mayor Parrish inquired whether renovated buildings or additions to existing structures would be required to apply to the Committee of Adjustment. Mayor Parrish thanked Planning staff for the report and advised that it would be forwarded to the Mayor' s Housing Task Force. In response, Jordan Lee, Manager, Zoning Initiatives, advised that alterations to existing buildings would not require an application to the Committee of Adjustment.
RECOMMENDATION PDC-0011-2026
Moved By Mayor Parrish

That an implementing zoning by-law amendment be enacted at a future City Council meeting.
That notwithstanding planning protocol, that this report regarding the proposed amendments to Zoning By-law 0225-2007, be considered both the public meeting and a combined information and recommendation report.
