Item 10.2 Tony Jasinski, Mississauga Majors Baseball Association
Tony Jasinski, Mississauga Majors Baseball Association ("Mississauga Majors"), spoke in opposition to staff's recommendation with respect to implementing prescribed Outdoor Sport User Group Permit Fee strategy for the Mississauga Majors Baseball Association.
In response to Councillor Dasko's enquiry, Tony Jasinski advised that the Mississauga Majors is comprised of all volunteers and has partnered with the City and school boards over the years.
In response to Councillor Kovac's enquiry on when the Mississauga Majors was incorporated, Tony Jasinski explained the partnership with other baseball associations and the subsequent incorporation of the Mississauga Majors in 2005. Mr. Jasinski further explained that in past negotiations, all baseball associations participated in discussions regarding waived fees and were aware that the Mississauga Majors would not be subject to fees and they were encouraged to participate in capital investments.
In response to Councillor Horneck's enquiry regarding how the city handles requests from sports groups who wish to make investments to sports fields/spaces, Nadia Paladino, Director Parks & Forestry & Environment explained that this information will be available in an upcoming report to Council on how to approach partnerships moving forward.
Item 10.2 was moved up for discussion and vote.
Councillor Dasko raised a concern that the past informal agreements between the City and the Mississauga Majors were made on the proviso that the waived fees would be honoured and moved a Motion to have the report referred to staff to work with the Mississauga Majors Baseball Association on a path forward.
Raj Sheth, Commissioner, Community Services advised that staff did meet with the representatives of the Mississauga Majors, and that this report is a means to reconcile past issues and that the report that will be brought to Council at a later date to deal with partnerships moving forward.
In response to Mayor Parrish's enquiry regarding a possible change to the staff's recommendation if the report is referred back to staff, Raj Sheth, Commissioner, Community Services advised that it is unlikely the recommendation will change as this report reconciles the subsidies that the Mississauga Majors have received in the past.
RECOMMENDATION GC-0075-2026

In response to Councillor Tedjo's enquiry regarding how do we address windrows that are created on the roads that are connected to paths, Helen Noehammer, Director, Works Operations & Maintenance advised that the connections from park trails or sidewalks, or street to street pedestrian connections, are not part of the residential driveway windrow clearing program; however, it is part of the sidewalk or walkway pathway clearing program and adhere to those standard timelines.

OTHER BUSINESS/ANNOUNCEMENTS
Mayor Parrish advised that the city will be receiving its third instalment from the federal government's housing accelerated fund, and enquired whether this instalment has a positive impact on the Development Charges (DC) balance, Marisa Chiu, Commissioner of Corporate Services, Chief Financial Officer and Treasurer explained that the second instalment went towards offsetting the 2025 DC reduction shortfalls, and that a portion of the third instalment will help with the 2026 DC reduction shortfalls; however, those numbers will not be known until November 2026.
In response to Mayor Parrish's enquiry regarding the influx of building applications, Andrew Whittemore, Commissioner, Planning and Building explained the number of units under construction, new starts, approved for permits and the number of approved units as a result of Council's support through the planning process of Official Plan Amendments and Rezonings, and that the Housing Task Force has noted the positive impact that Council's incentives has had.
Mayor Parrish spoke to the importance of DC review of the whole province and lobbying other levels of government demonstrating what we have done as a result of having received funding.
Councillor Butt advised that the City of Burlington has passed a Resolution on DC relief that goes beyond November 14, 2026 and requested that staff provide confirmation that this was done legally and if so, provide Council with legal advice to extend our DC relief program beyond November 14, 2026.
Members of General Committee spoke to events and celebrations taking place in Mississauga.
