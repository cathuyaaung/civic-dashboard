PUBLIC MEETING RECOMMENDATION REPORT (WARD 9)
1. Recommendation Report - OZ-OPA 24-15 W 9 - 5100 Erin Mills Parkway - 0006-2026.pdf

3. Item 6.1 Staff Presentation OZ OPA 24-15 W9.pdf
Official Plan Amendment and Rezoning applications to permit a multi-phased development consisting of eight apartment buildings and nine towers with building heights ranging from 20 to 44 storeys, an open space and public park with a total of 3,162 apartment units
Address: 5100 Erin Mills Parkway
Owner: EMTC Holdings Inc.
File: OZ/OPA 24-15 W9
Jennifer Renaud- Nicholson, Planner, provided an overview of the timelines and purpose of the report, subject lands and the proposal.
Councillor Reid addressed the Committee regarding the consultation process, noting that while outreach to the public and students occurred, the proposal does not respect the needs of the surrounding neighbourhood.Councillor stated that significant gaps remain and that the proposal, as presented, is not suitable for the area.
Councillors B. Butt and S. McFadden echoed the local Councillor’s comments, stating that the proposal was not appropriate for this neighbourhood, emphasizing the need to advocate strongly for this site.
Junaid Zikar, a local resident, expressed appreciation that Councillors understand the community’s concerns and thanked Planning staff for their work. J. Zikar noted that the applicant chose not to file a re-submission, thereby bypassing an important step and failing to work collaboratively with the City and community. J. Zikar stressed the need for meaningful engagement, acknowledging residents’ understanding of housing needs.
J. Zikar inquired if the City intended to negotiate building height or refuse the application entirely and whether Council would authorize Legal Services to oppose the appeal at the OLT.
Chris Rouse, Director, Development and Design, responded that mediation is typically undertaken, and if a settlement was reached, the matter would return to Council for an in‑camera presentation. C. Rouse stated that staff would review the full application package, including height and advised that if the application remains unchanged, Planning staff’s position would be one of non‑support.
Aiden Stanley, Executive Manager, Development North, stated that the applicant has not submitted materials addressing the concerns raised.
Councillor Reid thanked Mr. Zikar for his comments and noted that a town hall will be held regarding Ward 9 developments. Councillor Reid confirmed that staff will continue to provide information to support the public’s understanding of the planning process.
RECOMMENDATION PDC-0003-2026
Moved By Councillor M. Reid
That City Council direct Legal Services, representatives from the appropriate City Departments and any necessary consultants to oppose the subject applications under File OZ/OPA 24-15 W9 to permit a multi-phased development consisting of eight apartment buildings and nine towers with building heights ranging from 20 to 44 storeys, an open space and public park with a total of 3,162 apartment units before the Ontario Land Tribunal (OLT) consistent with the recommendations outlined in the report dated January 7, 2026 from the Commissioner of Plan ning and Building, that concludes that the proposed official plan amendment and rezoning are not acceptable from a planning standpoint and should not be approve d.
That City Council provide the Planning and Building Department with the authority to instruct Legal Services on modifications to the position deemed necessary during or before the Ontario Land Tribunal hearing process, however , if there is a potential for settlement then a report shall be brought back to Council by Legal Services.
That one oral submission be received.
YES (9)
Councillor S. Dasko, Councillor C. Fonseca, Councillor J. Kovac, Councillor M. Mahoney, Councillor S. McFadden, Councillor J. Horneck, Councillor M. Reid, Councillor B. Butt, and Councillor N. Hart
ABSENT (2)
Councillor D. Damerla, and Councillor A. Tedjo
Carried (9 to 0)

Economic Development Strategy, Path to Prosperity 2030
1. Economic Development Strategy, Path to Prosperity 2030 - 0051-2026.pdf
2. Appendix 1 Economic Development Strategy Path to Prosperity 2030.pdf
3. Appendix 2 Path to Prosperity 2030 Action Implementation Plan.pdf
4. Appendix 3 Path to Prosperity 2030 Stakeholder Participant List.pdf
5. Item 6.3 Staff Presentation - Economic Development Strategy.pdf
Christina Kakaflikas, Director Economic Development, provided an overview of the Division’s recent achievements, the Path to Prosperity 2030 strategy, and its strategic pillars. C. Kakaflikas outlined the implementation plan, external funding resources, partnerships, and the communication plan.
C. Kakaflikas thanked Economic Development Office (EDO) staff and Members of Council for their continued partnership.
John Barber, Supervisor, Economic Analysis & Programs Development, presented further details on the strategic pillars and associated objectives, including strengthening economic diversification and agility, fostering innovation and sector‑led growth, investing in future‑ready talent, and supporting vibrant and competitive communities. J. Barber highlighted the importance of priority sectors within the strategy.
Mayor Parrish thanked staff for the presentation.
Councillor Martin Reid requested clarification on the $7.3M tax impact and 954 jobs, and inquired about expanding talent development, including co‑op partnerships and enhanced collaboration with Sheridan College. C. Kakaflikas noted that annual investment and job targets are set internally, figures reflect companies that were directly supported, and that the City aims to close gaps between industry needs and post‑secondary programming.
Councillor Stephen Dasko inquired about the integration with other City divisions, noting opportunities with arts, culture, museums, and Visit Mississauga. C. Kakaflikas explained that further alignment is a priority; for example, transportation remains a key focus area, and better coordination of transit investments with employment needs would support economic objectives. Additional opportunities exist with local retailers.
Councillor Brad Butt emphasized support for strengthening the local economy and inquired regarding the strategies to attract businesses to Business Improvement Areas (BIAs). C. Kakaflikas noted that BIAs will play a key role in implementation.
Councillor Natalie Hart raised concerns regarding retention of medium‑sized enterprises. John Barber and Christina Kakaflikas outlined outreach methods, including surveys and targeted marketing and added that staff are always willing to connect businesses to available resources when opportunities arise.
Councillor Chris Fonseca inquired about opportunities to leverage federal and provincial funding, including programs outside traditional funding streams, opportunities for youth engagement, such as programs at the Glenforest Youth Hub, and support for non‑profit organizations.
C. Kakaflikas explained that the team maintains strong knowledge of funding programs and eligibility requirements and works with businesses to understand their growth plans and direct them accordingly. J. Barber noted that the Economic Division has a dedicated youth‑oriented initiative in collaboration with school boards, supporting opportunities in manufacturing and entrepreneurship. J. Barber emphasized the importance of ongoing outreach to connect businesses with available programs.
RECOMMENDATION PDC-0005-2026
Moved By Councillor N. Hart
That the report entitled ‘Economic Development Strategy, Path to Prosperity 2030’ dated January 7, 2026, be approved by the Planning and Development Committee.
That staff be authorized to make non-substantive stylistic and technical changes to finalize Appendix 1: Economic Development Strategy, Path to Prosperity 2030 for publishing.

RECOMMENDATION REPORT (ALL WARDS)
1. Recommendation Report - Mississauga's Retail Strategy - 0520-2025.pdf
2. Appendix 1 - Mississauga Retail Strategy_FINAL.pdf
3. Appendix 2 - Implementation Table.pdf
4. Item 6.4 Staff Presentation - Mississauga Retail Strategy.pdf
Mississauga’s Retail Strategy
Liza Toliao, Advisor, Economic Development Initiatives, introduced the Retail Strategy.
Chris White, Consultant, Parcel Economics, presented an overview of current retail challenges, opportunities, key goals, future context, retail gaps and matchmaking, priority actions, and monitoring strategies.
Councillor Kovac commented on parking pressures in downtown Mississauga, impacts of reduced store sizes post‑COVID‑19, inquired about the potential for adding retail categories to BIA awards and timelines for Phase 2 of the Retail Strategy. C. White responded to the questions.
Councillor Reid thanked staff and consultants, and inquired about how placemaking principles would apply across the city. C. White stated that placemaking can take various forms and be tailored to each area and scale.
Councillor Dasko inquired regarding the level of engagement with BIAs (Business Improvement Areas). C. White confirmed direct engagement and indicated Parcel Economics would continue incorporating BIA feedback.
Mayor Parrish noted the unique character of Wards 1 and 11 and expressed support for increased mixed retail and small‑scale stores within residential areas.
Councillor Butt thanked staff and noted the importance of including smaller neighbourhood retail areas, referencing BIA consultations.
Councillor Fonseca inquired how the traditional retail areas in wards without BIAs, such as Ward 3, could be supported. Councillor Fonseca requested follow‑up with the Ward 3 office regarding neighbourhood placemaking.
RECOMMENDATION PDC-0006-2026
Moved By Councillor M. Reid
That the Mississauga Retail Strategy, attached as Appendix 1 to the report dated January 7, 2026 titled “Mississauga’s Retail Strategy” from the Commissioner of Planning and Building, be approved and published on the City of Mississauga’s website;
That staff be authorized to make non-substantive stylistic and technical changes to finalize the Retail Strategy for publishing; and,
That a oneyear pilot be launched to begin implementing the Retail Strategy, supported by a noncomplement position funded through the Retail Strategy capital project budget. This position will also research and define the resources needed to sustain new service delivery.
That staff report back through the business planning and budget process with a recommended structure to support ongoing service delivery, including stakeholder collaboration, business engagement, market research, and program design to strengthen the retail sector.

Councillor S. Dasko, Councillor C. Fonseca, Councillor J. Kovac, Councillor M. Mahoney, Councillor S. McFadden, Councillor J. Horneck, Councillor M. Reid, Councillor B. Butt, and Councillor N. Hart
ABSENT (2)
Councillor D. Damerla, and Councillor A. Tedjo

4. Item 6.5 Staff Presentation Office Community Improvement Plans.pdf
Office Commun ity Improvement Plans
Bindu Shah, Manager, Housing Policy and Development Incentives provided an overview of the Downtown Community Improvement Plans, current state, details of the proposal, eligibility criteria, application process and next steps.
Councillor Butt inquired about provisions for mixed-use developments, asking whether the new plan will recognize such projects and whether developers would receive associated incentives. B. Shah confirmed that the incentive would apply to the commercial component of mixed-use developments.
Councillor Kovac questioned the timeline of the plan and how aggressive the additional incentives were and noted that the increased flexibility was reassuring. B. Shah responded that the addition of this incentive has been well received. Economic staff remain optimistic about the office development landscape and believe the changes will be beneficial. Jason Bevan, Director City Planning Strategies, added that the by-law includes a standard five-year window and a square footage cap.
Councillor J. Horneck inquired whether the success of such projects depended on securing a collection of tenants or an anchor tenant, and whether incentives should be offered to proponents who already have a defined plan. J. Bevan explained that securing at least one tenant is generally required to move a project forward. For example, a five-storey building would allow greater flexibility, and incentives would support both financial modelling and project financing. J. Bevan noted that international companies considering Mississauga will assess available office space or opportunities to build new modern office space.
Mayor Parrish asked whether there were comparable examples of buildings in downtown Brampton and requested clarification regarding the Regional Official Plan. Mayor Parrish expressed support for Councillor Butt’s comments regarding Highway 10 in Ward 5 and for the concept of living and working within the same building. Mayor Parrish thanked staff for their work. B. Shah concluded by noting that there were applications currently supported through the Regional Official Plan, and that the Region’s team was examining Mississauga’s incentives as part of their review.
RECOMMENDATION PDC-0007-2026
Moved By Councillor J. Kovac
That any submissions made at the public meeting held on January 26, 2026 be received for information.
That City Council enact by-laws substantially in accordance with Appendices 1 and 2 of the report titled “Office Community Improvement Plans” dated January 7, 2026 from the Commissioner of Planning and Building that incorporate changes from the January 26, 2026 public meeting, if necessary, without the requirement for further notice.
That by-law 0136-2017 “A by-law to delegate approval authority regarding incentive programs within the Downtown Community Improvement Plan” be repealed.
That the City Clerk forward the report titled “Office Community Improvement Plans” dated January 7, 2026 from the Commissioner of Planning and Building, and its appendices, to the Region of Peel to encourage them to match the proposed office incentives.
That staff analyse the merit of incentivizing hotel and cultural and creative space uses in the Downtown and Hurontario Growth Area Community Improvement Project Areas.
